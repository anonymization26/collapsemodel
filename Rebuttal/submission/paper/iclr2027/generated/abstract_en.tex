Selecting a training combination from fixed source-data pools can require hundreds of supervised fits. We study how target feature geometry screens these combinations and how early validation scores guide subsequent fits. Our pool-query policy estimates additive effects from overlapping combinations. With exact additivity and exact observations, 15 independent queries identify the validation-loss surface for all triples from 15 pools. In fresh DomainNet logistic evaluations, 25 fits yield mean test Brier of 0.916072, versus 0.919060 for fixed second-moment matching at 50 fits and 0.916078 for exhaustive validation at 455 fits. The same-budget comparisons show a smaller advantage over incidence-based experimental design. With resident features and four fitting workers, the two-batch 25-fit policy selects a model in 1.154 seconds versus 2.069 seconds for fixed matching at 50 fits. On Optdigits, changing the partition of the same examples can sharply degrade the additive approximation and the selected model. Together with the shared-linear-model analysis of target-weighted screening, these results characterize when fewer fits preserve selection quality and when feature costs and loss interactions limit the savings.
