Given $M$ frozen-representation data pools, which candidates can be shortlisted without labels before expensive evaluation? We separate exact spectral claims from empirical screening. For fixed, label-independent preprocessing and declared source weights, the merged spectrum is exactly determined by a sum of positive-semidefinite Gram operators. Under explicit block-compatible singular directions this sum has an exact local $2 \times 2$ decomposition; a constructive counterexample shows that marginal spectra and principal angles—and hence the earlier four-scalar summary—do not determine merged effective rank in general. Our primary Stage-1 method is a composable rank-$L$ PSD Gram sketch. Transmitted tail statistics give deterministic Fannes–Audenaert intervals for log-effective-rank error and a sufficient certificate for one Full-Gram greedy step. Controlled sweeps across four encoders show that all 2,880 selected-prefix intervals cover the exact score, but none of 7,200 greedy steps is certified for $L \leq 50$, and DPP is at least as competitive. In 28 held-out encoder-target units, even validation-oracle source-supervised adapters trail frozen identity by $0.067$ (target-cluster 95% CI $[-0.098,-0.044]$); small shortlist regret is therefore not downstream benefit. We make no global-optimality, privacy, practical-compression, or downstream-benefit claim. Code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/collapsemodel-1DB6.
