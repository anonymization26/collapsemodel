\begin{abstract}
Given $M$ candidate datasets, which $k{\ll}M$ subset best preserves
representational diversity when their features are merged---without
access to labels or downstream tasks?
We address this \emph{label-free data-pool compression} problem
by deriving the \textbf{Collapse Model}, a closed-form bridge
between spectral geometry and compression decisions.
The model predicts the merged effective rank ($\reff$) from a
$2{\times}2$ Gram-block eigenstructure via two compression-relevant
SVD channels (energy ratio~$\gamma$, directional alignment~$\alpha$),
yielding an explicit superadditivity threshold $\rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)$
that drives a greedy compression algorithm using only
\emph{per-dataset summary statistics}---suitable for
privacy-constrained or streaming settings where features cannot be
shared. Across 427 real feature-matrix pairs from 5 encoder families and
2 modalities the formula attains Pearson $r{=}0.97$.
Collapse-guided compression is near-optimal (${\leq}1\%$ regret
vs.\ exhaustive search), matches the closest published
spectral-diversity baseline (Vendi-Greedy) on retention while
requiring only summary statistics rather than full feature matrices,
and strictly outperforms Facility Location / $k$-Medoids on
downstream linear-probe accuracy (sign test $p{<}10^{-3}$,
Collapse exceeds Random's $95\%$ CI on the mean LP).
The comparison reveals two distinct LP-relevant diversity axes
(\emph{spectral retention} captured by Collapse and
\emph{centroid coverage} captured by farthest-first traversal),
clarifying when each signal dominates.
The framework scales to $M{=}43$ pools at up to $14{\times}$
compression and identifies a quantitative supervised /
self-supervised spectral-geometry divide via a bimodal
principal-angle distribution exposed in CLIP and DINO encoders.
Code is available at \url{https://github.com/anonymization26/collapsemodel}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern pretrained models increasingly rely on combining features from many
candidate datasets---multi-domain transfer learning, data curation for
self-supervised pretraining \citep{gadre2023datacomp},
and continual learning all face the question:
\emph{given $M$ candidate datasets, which $k{\ll}M$ subset preserves the
diversity of the full pool when their features are concatenated?}
We call this the \emph{label-free data-pool compression} problem:
unlike classical coreset selection \citep{mirzasoleiman2020coresets}
and recent \emph{sample-level} data curation methods
(DSIR~\citep{xie2023dsir}, DsDm~\citep{engstrom2024dsdm},
D$^2$ Pruning~\citep{maharana2023d2pruning}),
we seek compression at the \emph{dataset level}
(not the sample level), without access to labels or downstream tasks.
The criterion is representational diversity, formalized as the
effective rank $\reff(H)=\exp(-\sum_i p_i\log p_i)$ of the merged features
\citep{roy2007effective,garrido2023rankme}.
No prior work provides a closed-form answer to how $\reff$ changes
under feature concatenation,
forcing practitioners to rely on exhaustive search or heuristics
that scale poorly with $M$.

Prior work addresses related but distinct problems.
\emph{Spectral diversity metrics} such as RankMe~\citep{garrido2023rankme}, VNE~\citep{kim2023vne}, and the Vendi Score~\citep{friedman2023vendi,pasarkar2024cousins} quantify the diversity of a single set via entropy of a singular-value or similarity-matrix spectrum; they do not predict how two sets' diversity combines under concatenation.
\emph{Label-free coreset selection} (ELFS~\citep{shen2025elfs}, ZCore~\citep{griffin2024zcore}, DEFT-UCS~\citep{das2024deft}) selects a subset of \emph{samples} using geometry or proxy dynamics; it is complementary but orthogonal to our dataset-level formulation.
\emph{Pool-level curation} (DataComp~\citep{gadre2023datacomp}, DataComp-LM~\citep{li2024dclm}, DsDm~\citep{engstrom2024dsdm}, Quad~\citep{zhang2025quad}) uses filtering or model-aware heuristics without a closed-form diversity criterion.
\emph{Representation similarity measures} (SVCCA \citep{raghu2017svcca}, PWCCA \citep{morcos2018pwcca}, CKA \citep{kornblith2019similarity}, PARC \citep{bolya2021parc}) compare two representations but do not predict merged rank.
\emph{Transferability estimators} (LogME \citep{zhang2021logme}, H-score \citep{bao2019hscore}, TransRate \citep{huang2022transrate}, LEEP \citep{li2021nleep}, reassessed by \citet{ibrahim2022newer}) and PMI-based dataset valuation~\citep{zhang2024pmi} predict downstream accuracy but either require labels or do not model merging.
Task2Vec \citep{achille2019task2vec} avoids labels but requires probe fine-tuning.
Model-merging methods (Task Arithmetic~\citep{ilharco2023task}, TIES-Merging~\citep{yadav2023ties}, DARE~\citep{yu2024dare}) combine parameters, not features.
Intrinsic-dimensionality work \citep{pope2021intrinsic,ansuini2019intrinsic} characterizes individual matrices but not merged ones.
To our knowledge, no existing work provides either a closed-form prediction of $\reff([H_A;H_B])$ or a theoretical framework that explains \emph{when and why} feature merging increases diversity.

We derive the \textbf{Collapse Model}, a closed-form bridge between spectral geometry and label-free compression.
It expresses the merged effective rank as a function of four per-dataset quantities---$r_A$, $r_B$, the energy ratio $\gamma=\|H_B\|_*/\|H_A\|_*$, and the subspace alignment $\alpha=\SAk(H_A,H_B)$---via a $2\times2$ Gram block eigenstructure.
Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} gives exact eigenvalues under the principal-angle model (discriminant $D$, weight $r^*$); Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} approximates the merged spectrum as a two-component mixture with closed-form entropy.
The resulting formula identifies a superadditivity threshold $\rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)$ and partitions the $(\alpha,\gamma)$ plane into \textbf{three behaviour regimes} (Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}): \emph{superadditive} (balanced energy and low alignment, $r_{\mathrm{dom}}/r_{\mathrm{sub}}<\rho_c$), \emph{directional collapse} ($\alpha\to1$, subspaces overlap so merging adds no diversity), and \emph{energy collapse} ($\gamma\gg1$ or $\gamma\ll1$, one dataset dominates and the gain vanishes).
Our $\SAk$ uses mean squared cosine of principal angles, which connects more directly to projection theory than SVCCA's unsigned cosine or PWCCA's energy-weighted form;
the $U$-channel uses the Two-NN intrinsic dimension estimator \citep{facco2017estimating}.

\textbf{Why closed-form compression?}
Exhaustive search over $M$ candidates requires $\binom{M}{k}$ merged
SVDs at $O(Nd^2)$ each, with simultaneous access to all feature matrices
($M{=}14, k{=}5$: $2002$ SVDs; $M{=}100, k{=}10$: $1.7{\times}10^{13}$).
The Collapse Model reduces this to $O(kM)$ rank predictions from
per-dataset summary statistics, enabling compression in
privacy-constrained and streaming settings.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~\emph{Problem formulation}---\emph{label-free data-pool compression}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:compression}): an $\mathcal{O}(kM)$ greedy algorithm
operating from per-dataset summary statistics
($\reff,\|\cdot\|_*,V_{\mathrm{top}\text{-}k}$),
suitable for privacy-constrained and streaming settings where
feature matrices cannot be shared.
(2)~\emph{Closed-form theory} (Lemma~\ref{lem:gram},
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}, Theorem~\ref{thm:error}):
the first closed-form predictor of merged $\reff$, decomposing it into
three orthogonal SVD channels with a superadditivity threshold
$\rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}) that classifies
merging into three regimes (superadditive / directional collapse /
energy collapse) and an explicit multiplicative error bound.
(3)~\emph{Two-signal decomposition of LP-relevant diversity}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:compression}): benchmarking against modern baselines
reveals that downstream-relevant diversity factors into two orthogonal
signals---\emph{spectral retention} (captured by Collapse, $r_A{+}r_B$,
Vendi-Greedy) and \emph{centroid coverage} (captured by farthest-first
traversal). A two-stage Cen$\to$Col hybrid composes both signals and
dominates pure Collapse on \emph{both} LP and retention simultaneously
at $k{=}10$ (Appendix~\ref{app:p2e_hybrid}), positioning the closed-form
formula as a composable primitive rather than a standalone strategy.

\textbf{Empirical findings.}
The formula is validated on 280 controlled + 120 non-principal-angle
synthetic + 427 real pairs across 5 encoder families and 2 modalities
(Pearson $r{=}0.97$, $R^2_{\mathrm{cal}}{=}0.95$).
The framework identifies a quantitative supervised/self-supervised
spectral-geometry divide: ResNet-50 achieves $100\%$ superadditivity
across 91 pairs, while CLIP and DINO reach only $29$--$36\%$ on
cross-domain merges---a gap mechanistically explained by a bimodal
principal-angle distribution that the model isolates as the responsible
mechanism.

Among label-free analyses, only the Collapse Model simultaneously
provides closed-form merged-rank prediction
($R^2_{\mathrm{cal}}{=}0.95$ on 427 pairs, Table~\ref{tab:e1d}),
behaviour-regime classification via $\rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)$, and
superadditivity diagnosis at the same $O(Nd\min(N,d))$ cost as
SVCCA/PWCCA; the naive $r_A{+}r_B$ matches Collapse in ordinal ranking
($\rho{=}0.963$ vs.\ $0.966$, not significant) but lacks absolute $R^2$, regime diagnosis, and redundancy detection.

\section{Background: SVD Three-Channel Decomposition}
\label{sec:background}

For any feature matrix $H\in\R^{N\times d}$ with thin SVD $H=U\Sigma V^\top$
($U\in\R^{N\times r}$, $\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_r>0)$,
$V\in\R^{d\times r}$), the decomposition separates three orthogonal aspects of representation.

\textbf{$\Sigma$ channel --- spectral energy.}
The \textbf{effective rank} summarizes spectral distribution:
\begin{equation}
  \reff(H) = \exp\!\left(-\sum_{i=1}^r p_i \log p_i\right),
  \quad p_i = \frac{\sigma_i}{\sum_j \sigma_j},
  \label{eq:reff}
\end{equation}
where $p_i$ is the singular-value share (not squared), so $r_{\mathrm{eff}}\in[1,r]$.
The nuclear norm $\|H\|_*=\sum_i\sigma_i$ captures total energy.
A spectrum-difference quantity (Spectral Transfer Gap, used in the
Gram-block transfer bound of Appendix~\ref{app:proof_prop1})
is defined there.

\textbf{$V$ channel --- directional structure.}
Right singular vectors $V$ encode which directions are activated.
\begin{definition}[Subspace Alignment]
\label{def:sa}
Let $\hat{V}_A^k,\hat{V}_B^k\in\R^{d\times k}$ be the top-$k$ right singular
vectors of $H_A,H_B$.
The \textbf{Subspace Alignment} at level $k$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \SAk(H_A,H_B) = \frac{1}{k}\norm{\hat{V}_A^{k\top}\hat{V}_B^k}_F^2
  = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^k\cos^2\theta_i,
  \label{eq:sak}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_1\leq\cdots\leq\theta_k$ are the principal angles.
\end{definition}
$\SAk\in[0,1]$: $\SAk=0$ means perfectly orthogonal subspaces (maximum complementarity);
$\SAk=1$ means identical subspaces (no new directions from merging).

\textbf{$U$ channel --- sample geometry.}
Left singular vectors $U\in\R^{N\times r}$ encode the geometric distribution of samples.
The \textbf{U-space Intrinsic Dimension (\UID)} via the Two-NN estimator:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{UID}(H) = \frac{1}{\log 2\cdot\mathbb{E}[\log(\mu_2/\mu_1)]},
  \label{eq:uid}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_1,\mu_2$ are geodesic distances to the nearest and second-nearest neighbors
on the unit sphere $S^{k-1}$ (L2-normalized $U_k$ rows).

\textbf{Role of each channel.}
$\gamma$ and $\alpha$ enter the merged-rank prediction formula
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method}) and drive the compression algorithm.
\UID\ characterizes the intrinsic geometry of individual datasets
and is used for encoder diagnostics (Sec.~\ref{sec:e1d}) but
does \emph{not} appear in the prediction formula; it is included
here to complete the orthogonal decomposition of representation space.

\textbf{Independence.}
Pairwise Spearman tests (Bonferroni-corrected, $n{=}15$ per probe) detect
no significant correlation among the three channels on ResNet-50 or
ViT-B/16 (all $p_{\mathrm{Bonf}}{>}0.38$), consistent with
$\gamma,\alpha,\UID$ capturing distinct properties.

\section{The Collapse Model}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $H_A\in\R^{N_A\times d}$ and $H_B\in\R^{N_B\times d}$ be L2-row-normalized feature matrices
with effective ranks $r_A=\reff(H_A)$, $r_B=\reff(H_B)$,
nuclear norms $\|H_A\|_*$, $\|H_B\|_*$, and subspace alignment $\alpha=\SAk(H_A,H_B)$.
Define:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma = \frac{\|H_B\|_*}{\|H_A\|_*} \in (0,\infty),
  \quad
  r_{\mathrm{dom}} = \begin{cases}r_A & \gamma\leq1 \\ r_B & \gamma>1\end{cases},
  \quad
  r_{\mathrm{sub}} = \begin{cases}r_B & \gamma\leq1 \\ r_A & \gamma>1\end{cases}.
\end{equation}
$\gamma$ is the \textbf{energy ratio} of the two matrices;
$r_{\mathrm{dom}}$ is the effective rank of the higher-energy matrix.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:theorem}

Our derivation has two layers: an \emph{exact} algebraic result (Lemma~\ref{lem:gram})
and an \emph{approximate} prediction formula (Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse})
that applies the exact result via a spectral mixture model.

\begin{lemma}[Exact: 2$\times$2 Gram Block Eigenstructure]
\label{lem:gram}
Under the \emph{principal-angle model} where the $i$-th right singular vector of $H_B$
lies in $\mathrm{span}\{V_{A,i},V_{\perp,i}\}$ with $V_{A,i}^\top V_{B,i}=\cos\theta_i$,
the $2\times2$ block of the Gram matrix $G=H_A^\top H_A+H_B^\top H_B$ in the
$(V_{A,i},V_{\perp,i})$ basis has eigenvalues
$\sigma_{A,i}^2(A\pm D_i)/2$ with $A=1+\gamma^2$ and
\begin{equation}
  D = \sqrt{(1+\gamma^2)^2 - 4\gamma^2(1-\alpha)}
    = \sqrt{(1-\gamma^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\,\alpha},
  \label{eq:discriminant}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha=\cos^2\theta$ is the (shared) squared alignment.
The nuclear-norm fraction of the dominant merged component is
\begin{equation}
  r^* = \frac{\sqrt{(A+D)/2}}{\sqrt{(A+D)/2} + \sqrt{(A-D)/2}}.
  \label{eq:rstar}
\end{equation}
This result is \emph{exact} under the stated model (proof: Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm}, Part A).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse Model: Predicted Merged Rank]
\label{prop:collapse}
Under the two-component spectral mixture approximation (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm}, Part B),
the predicted merged effective rank is:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}} =
  \exp\!\Big(H_b(r^*) + r^*\log r_{\mathrm{dom}} + (1-r^*)\log r_{\mathrm{sub}}\Big),
  \label{eq:pred}
\end{equation}
where $r^*$ is from Lemma~\ref{lem:gram},
$H_b(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)$ is binary entropy,
and $r_{\mathrm{dom}},r_{\mathrm{sub}}$ are the effective ranks of the higher- and
lower-energy matrices.
The approximation error is $O(\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max})$, controlled by $\gamma$.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Interpretation.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:pred} is a weighted log-geometric mean of the
individual ranks plus a balance bonus $H_b(r^*)$ (maximal when
$r^*{=}0.5$, vanishing as $r^*{\to}0,1$).

\textbf{Behavior regimes.}
\emph{Superadditive} ($\alpha{\approx}0$, $\gamma{\approx}1$): $r^*{\approx}0.5$,
$\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}{\approx}2\sqrt{r_Ar_B}\geq r_{\mathrm{dom}}$.
\emph{Directional collapse} ($\alpha{\to}1$): $r^*{\to}1$, $H_b(r^*){\to}0$,
$\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}{\to}r_{\mathrm{dom}}$.
\emph{Energy collapse} ($\gamma{\gg}1$ or $\gamma{\ll}1$): $r^*{\to}1$,
$\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}{\to}r_{\mathrm{dom}}$.
When $\alpha{\to}1$ \emph{and} $\gamma{\gg}1$, the merged rank can fall
\emph{below} $r_{\mathrm{sub}}$---a net loss in diversity.

\subsection{Superadditivity Threshold}
\label{sec:superadd}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded-Ratio Superadditivity]
\label{cor:superadd}
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} predicts $\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}>r_{\mathrm{dom}}$
if and only if
\begin{equation}
  \frac{r_{\mathrm{dom}}}{r_{\mathrm{sub}}} < \rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)
  \;\equiv\; \exp\!\!\left(\frac{H_b(r^*)}{1-r^*}\right),
  \label{eq:rho_c}
\end{equation}
where $r^*$ is from Lemma~\ref{lem:gram}.
The threshold $\rho_c$ is increasing in $\alpha$:
$\rho_c\geq4.0$ for all $\alpha\in[0,0.9]$ at $\gamma=1$.
At $\alpha=1$: $H_b(r^*)\to0$, $\rho_c\to1$---superadditivity is impossible.
\end{corollary}

\textbf{Physical meaning.}
$\rho_c$ is a rank-ratio threshold: orthogonal subspaces ($\alpha{\to}0$) allow
$\rho_c{\gg}1$ (very unbalanced pairs remain superadditive); identical subspaces
($\alpha{\to}1$) force $\rho_c{\to}1$ (no gain).
The two-part proof and principal-angle scope are in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm},~\ref{app:pa_scope}.

\subsection{Approximation Error Bound}
\label{sec:error_bound}

The two-component spectral mixture in Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}
replaces the per-dimension discriminants $D_i$ with a single value $\bar{D}$
computed from the average alignment $\bar\alpha=\SAk$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:error} formalizes when this approximation is exact
and how its error depends on observable quantities.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral Mixture Error Bound]
\label{thm:error}
Under the principal-angle model with per-dimension alignments
$\alpha_i=\cos^2\theta_i$, average alignment $\bar\alpha=\SAk$,
energy ratio $\gamma$, and condition number $\kappa=\sigma_1^A/\sigma_k^A$,
let $\hat{r}$ denote the Collapse Model prediction
(Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse})
and $r=\reff([H_A;H_B])$ the true merged effective rank.

\textbf{(a) Exactness under uniform alignment.}
If $\alpha_i=\bar\alpha$ for all $i=1,\ldots,k$, then $\hat{r}=r$.

\textbf{(b) General error bound.}
\begin{equation}
  \left|\log\frac{\hat{r}}{r}\right|
  \;\leq\;
  \frac{4\gamma^2}{\bar{D}^2}\cdot V_\alpha\cdot\log\kappa,
  \label{eq:error_bound}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{D}=\sqrt{(1-\gamma^2)^2+4\gamma^2\bar\alpha}$ is the average discriminant
and $V_\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^k p_i(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)^2$ is the
spectrum-weighted alignment variance with $p_i=\sigma_{A,i}/\|H_A\|_*$.
\end{theorem}

The three factors capture discriminant sensitivity, alignment
heterogeneity, and spectral non-uniformity respectively, and combine
to the worst-case guarantee $|\log(\hat r/r)|\leq\log\kappa$
(multiplicative error never exceeds the condition number).
Full proof and the discussion of why the formula generalises beyond the
principal-angle model (E1a-Ind, $r{=}0.9987$) are in
Appendices~\ref{app:error_proof},~\ref{app:generalization}.

\textbf{Calibration.}
On real data the formula acts as a systematic upper bound;
a single multiplicative constant $c$ restores quantitative accuracy and
follows a universal regression $c{=}0.946{-}0.175\log(N/\reff)$
across encoder families (Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}).
Because this is a \emph{one-parameter} linear model in the
observable $\log(N/\reff)$, a reliable estimate of $c$ requires
only $5$--$10$ labeled pairs per encoder family---a negligible
overhead compared to the cost of computing frozen-encoder features.
In privacy-constrained settings where even summary statistics from
a single reference pair are available, the per-encoder $c$ can be
estimated without exposing any raw features.
The same three channels also bound linear-probe transfer loss
(Appendix~\ref{app:proof_prop1}): low $\SAk$ implies \emph{high} merge
gain and \emph{high} transfer loss simultaneously.

\section{Formula Validation: Closing the Theoretical Loop}
\label{sec:experiments}

Before applying the Collapse Model to compression (Sec.~\ref{sec:applications}),
we establish a three-step validation chain:
(i)~\textbf{mathematical correctness}---the formula is exact under its
derivation assumptions (§\ref{sec:e1a_main}, synthetic grid);
(ii)~\textbf{real-data generalization}---the formula remains a strong
approximation across 5 encoder families, 14 vision datasets,
and 19 text corpora (§\ref{sec:e1d}, 427 pairs);
(iii)~\textbf{behavioral regime agreement}---empirical superadditivity
patterns match the three regimes predicted by
Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd} (§\ref{sec:e1d}, Table~\ref{tab:e1d}).
Together these close the theoretical loop: the formula is correct,
it generalizes, and its qualitative predictions are observable.
Additional controls (E1a-Ind 120 non-principal-angle pairs,
E1b 30 real pairs, E1c 57 pairs, E\_diverse cross-architecture,
E\_downstream $\reff$/LP-accuracy) all agree with the same picture
and are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:exp_extras}.
Feature matrices are L2-row-normalized and subsampled to
$N_{\mathrm{sub}}$ samples; effective rank uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:reff};
bootstrap CIs use $B=10{,}000$ resamples.

\label{sec:e1a_main}

280 controlled synthetic pairs generated under the principal-angle model
with $\alpha\in\{0,0.05,\ldots,1.0\}$ and $\gamma\in\{0.1,0.33,\ldots,10.0\}$
($d{=}512$, $N{=}1000$, 5 repeats per setting).

\subsection{Steps 2--3 --- Real-Data Generalization and Regime Agreement (E1d, 427 Pairs)}
\label{sec:e1d}

We next verify that the formula generalizes beyond its synthetic
derivation, and that its predicted three-regime behavior is empirically
observable.
The evaluation spans
5 encoder families (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, CLIP ViT-B/32, DINO ViT-S/16, SBERT),

\textbf{Generalization (Step 2).}
The formula remains a strong approximation on all 427 real-world pairs:
overall Pearson $r{=}0.970$ (uncalibrated, upper bound) and
$R^2_{\mathrm{cal}}{=}0.952$ after per-encoder-family calibration
(scatter visualization in Appendix~\ref{app:exp_extras}, Fig.~\ref{fig:hero_scatter}).
This confirms that the closed-form prediction, derived under the
idealized principal-angle model, transfers faithfully to real encoders
spanning supervised CNNs, ViT, contrastive/self-distillation transformers,
and sentence encoders.

\textbf{Regime agreement (Step 3).}
(i)~\textbf{ResNet-50 achieves 100\% superadditivity} across 91 pairs
(including medical and ImageNet), confirming the balanced-$(\gamma,\alpha)$
regime predicted by Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}.
(ii)~\textbf{Supervised/self-supervised divide} emerges exactly where
theory predicts: CLIP and DINO achieve only 29\%--36\% superadditivity,
with failures concentrating \emph{entirely} in cross-domain pairs
(handwritten$\times$ImageNet, natural$\times$digit fail universally)
while within-domain pairs remain near-perfect.
The root cause is a bimodal principal-angle distribution combined with
extreme condition numbers $\kappa{\in}[10^4,10^6]$
(Figure~\ref{fig:bimodal_structure}), mapping to the \emph{directional-collapse}
regime of Sec.~\ref{sec:superadd}.

\paragraph{Quantifying the bimodal gap.}
Per-probe calibrated $R^2$: CLIP $0.84>$ ResNet $0.76>$ DINO $0.55>$ ViT $0.49$;
ranking accuracy is uniformly high ($r{\geq}0.90$).
Figure~\ref{fig:bimodal_structure}(a,c) shows that CLIP/DINO's two-cluster
$\cos^2\theta_i$ distribution ($V_\alpha{>}0.07$) inflates the
$V_\alpha\log\kappa$ error term, explaining the upward bias in $\hat r$---the
failure mode is internally consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:error} rather than
a black-box surprise (full mechanism in Appendix~\ref{app:bimodal}).

(iii)~\textbf{Energy collapse} appears in ViT's 17 non-superadditive
pairs, all in $\gamma\notin[0.5,2]$---exactly the energy-imbalance
boundary of Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}.
(iv)~\textbf{Cross-modal transfer}: 171 SBERT text pairs
(98.8\% superadditive, ranking $r{=}0.793$) confirm that the
formula's \emph{ordinal} predictions are modality-agnostic.
Absolute calibration, however, requires modality-specific tuning:
the vision universal regression
($c{=}0.946{-}0.175\log(N/\reff)$, Appendix~\ref{app:calibration})
explains $R^2{=}0.96$ of vision variance but only $R^2{=}0.08$ of
SBERT variance, indicating that text encoders have a qualitatively
different spectral bias.
A text-specific calibration is left for future work.

\textbf{Loop closure.}
Steps 1--3 together establish that the Collapse Model is mathematically
exact, generalizes to real encoders, and reproduces the three regimes
(superadditive, directional collapse, energy collapse) predicted by
the theory. This licenses the applications developed next.
Cross-dataset calibration generalization, cross-probe consistency,
and $\reff$/LP-accuracy correspondence ($\rho{=}0.90$ within ViT family)
are in Appendix~\ref{app:exp_extras}.

\section{From Formula to Application: Label-Free Data-Pool Compression}
\label{sec:applications}
\label{sec:compression}
\label{sec:pool_selection}

Having closed the theoretical loop (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}),
we now demonstrate the concrete utility of the Collapse Model:
given $M$ candidate datasets and a target $D_t$, compress the pool
to $k{\ll}M$ while preserving $\reff$ of the merged representation.
Exhaustive search over $\binom{M}{k}$ subsets is intractable;
the Collapse Model enables compression at $O(kM)$ predictions from
per-dataset summary statistics.
At each round, \textbf{Collapse Greedy} scores every remaining candidate
by applying Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} to $(H_{\mathrm{cur}},H_{\mathrm{cand}})$
and selects the highest predicted $\reff$
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:collapse_greedy}).
We report two levels of evidence below:
(a)~a \emph{scale stress test} at $M{=}43$ showing the method holds at
heterogeneous scale (Table~\ref{tab:scale}), and
(b)~comparison against modern label-free baselines
(Table~\ref{tab:p1a_modern}); cross-architecture cross-modal results at
$M{=}14$ vision and $M{=}7$ text pools are in
Appendix~\ref{app:compression_m14}.

At each round Collapse Greedy scores each remaining candidate via
Prop.~\ref{prop:collapse} applied to $(H_{\mathrm{cur}},H_c)$---requiring
only $\SAk$ and $\gamma$ computed from per-dataset SVDs---and picks the
highest-predicted $\reff$
(pseudocode in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm}).
Baselines: \textbf{Max-$\reff$} (highest individual $\reff$, ignores $\alpha$);
\textbf{$\Ddir$-greedy} (most-orthogonal via $\Ddir=1-\SAk$, ignores $\gamma$);
\textbf{Random} (10 uniform subsets).
On a target-conditioned $M{=}10$ pool
(ResNet-50 features on CIFAR-10/100, STL-10, SVHN, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST,
BloodMNIST, DermaMNIST, PathMNIST, Oxford-Pets) at budgets $K{\in}\{1,2,3,5\}$,
\textbf{Collapse Greedy achieves $0\%$ regret at $K{\leq}3$} and $1.0\%$
at $K{=}5$---matching exhaustive search with $3\times$ speedup.
Max-$\reff$ drops to $67\%$ recall; $\Ddir$-greedy incurs $13$--$20\%$ regret.
All strategies produce identical downstream LP accuracy,%
\footnote{LP and $k$-NN rankings are strongly correlated: across
11 probes ${\times}$ 5 datasets (53 pairs),
Spearman $\rho(\mathrm{LP},\mathrm{kNN\text{-}5}){=}0.980$
($p{<}10^{-33}$).
We report LP throughout; see Appendix~\ref{app:e7_knn} for $k$-NN details.}
confirming that maximizing $\reff$ translates to downstream discriminability
(Appendix~\ref{app:e5_downstream}).

\paragraph{Compression at scale.}
Collapse-guided compression retains ${>}100\%$ of full-pool diversity
in every setting tested---a universal phenomenon enabled because
$\reff$ is not monotone in pool size: adding a redundant dataset shifts
the normalised spectrum toward its mode and \emph{decreases} entropy,
so removing redundancies via the $\alpha$ channel yields a compressed
pool of \emph{higher} diversity than the full pool.
On a $M{=}14$ vision pool Collapse outperforms Facility Location
($+7$--$14$\,pp) and $k$-Medoids ($+8$--$14$\,pp); on a $M{=}7$ text
pool (BERT/RoBERTa/GPT-2) it retains $103$--$106\%$
(full table in Appendix~\ref{app:compression_m14}).
On clean curated pools the $r_A{+}r_B$ and Max-$\reff$ heuristics match
Collapse within $0.7$\,pp at $M{=}43$ (Table~\ref{tab:scale})---a
predictable consequence of Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd} when
$\bar\alpha{<}0.2$.
The Collapse Model's role on such pools is to provide an
\emph{a priori} safety certificate
($\rho_c{>}4$ at $\bar\alpha{\leq}0.2$) and to take over when this
condition fails (clone attacks, fine-grained subset families, bimodal
cross-domain pairs); see Appendix~\ref{app:rsum_regime}.
At $k{=}7$ on $M{=}14$, Collapse Greedy runs in seconds versus
$\binom{14}{7}{=}3432$ exhaustive merged SVDs.

\paragraph{Two-signal decomposition and practical selection guide.}
Table~\ref{tab:p1a_modern} reveals that LP-relevant diversity decomposes
into two \emph{orthogonal} axes, each captured by a distinct family of
methods:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Spectral retention} (SR): Collapse, $r_A{+}r_B$,
    Max-$\reff$, and Vendi-Greedy all converge to
    $116$--$117\%$ retention---maximizing the entropy of the
    merged singular-value spectrum.
    This signal dominates at \emph{high compression ratios}
    ($k{\leq}7$, i.e., ${\geq}5.7{\times}$), where spectral steering
    determines which $k$ datasets cover the most directions.
  \item \textbf{Centroid coverage} (CC): Centroid-FF and DomainDiverse
    achieve higher LP at $k{\geq}7$ by spreading dataset
    \emph{mean embeddings} across semantic space, at a
    $17$--$22$\,pp retention cost.
    This signal dominates at \emph{low compression ratios}
    ($k$ near $M$), where random selection already saturates
    spectral diversity and distributional breadth becomes the
    bottleneck.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Practical guide.}
\emph{High compression} ($k{\leq}0.2M$, ${\geq}5{\times}$): use
Collapse (preferred when $\SAk{>}0.5$, rejecting clones $+25.5\%$;
Appendix~\ref{app:rsum_regime}) or $r_A{+}r_B$ on clean pools.
\emph{Low compression} ($k{>}0.2M$): use Centroid-FF or DomainDiverse.
\emph{Unknown regime}: the Cen$\to$Col hybrid
(Appendix~\ref{app:p2e_hybrid}) dominates on both SR and LP.

\paragraph{Ablation.}
On 256 vision pairs, the full model attains Spearman $\rho{=}0.966$
($r_A{+}r_B$: $0.963$; $\max(r_A,r_B)$: $0.953$; $1{-}\alpha$: $0.450$);
the $\Delta\rho{=}0.003$ gap reflects a low-$\alpha$/moderate-$\gamma$ degeneracy
where $\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}\!\approx\!c(r_A{+}r_B)$ on clean pools.
Calibrated ranking achieves $R^2{=}0.95$ and flags $74\%$ of non-superadditive pairs;
the clone confirms $\alpha$-channel rejection ($\reff{=}1251$ vs.\ $997$; Appendix~\ref{app:ablation_extra}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Collapse Model---a closed-form bridge from spectral
geometry to label-free data-pool compression.
Greedy compression retains ${>}100\%$ of full-pool $\reff$ across 6 probes
and 2 modalities, outperforming Facility Location and $k$-Medoids by
$+7$--$+14$\,pp and scaling to $M{=}43$ ($14.3{\times}$ compression,

Pearson $r{=}0.97$ on 427 real pairs ($R^2_{\mathrm{cal}}{=}0.95$),

supervised\,/\,SSL spectral-geometry divide.
The framework extends naturally to neural compression (attention-head
pruning, LoRA selection, MoE gating).

\section{Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}

The empirical calibration constant (Eq.~\ref{eq:universal_c})
reduces but does not eliminate per-encoder tuning.
The uniform-$\alpha$ assumption holds for supervised encoders and
within-domain self-supervised pairs ($r{>}0.90$), but fails on
cross-domain CLIP/DINO pairs (bimodal angles,
Fig.~\ref{fig:bimodal_structure}) and SBERT text pairs
(universal-$c$ explains $r{=}0.08$ of variance vs.\ $0.96$ for vision);
ranking remains correct while absolute predictions are biased.
A piecewise model for non-uniform $V_\alpha$ is left as future work.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} and Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}}
\label{app:proof_thm}

\textbf{Part A: Exact $2\times2$ eigenvalue algebra.}

Under the principal-angle model, the $i$-th right singular vector of $H_B$ is
$V_{B,i}=\cos\theta_i V_{A,i}+\sin\theta_i V_{\perp,i}$,
where $V_{A,i}$ is the $i$-th right singular vector of $H_A$
and $V_{\perp,i}\perp V_{A,i}$.
In the basis $\{V_{A,i},V_{\perp,i}\}$:
\begin{align}
  H_A^\top H_A \text{ (block)} &= \sigma_{A,i}^2\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}, \\
  H_B^\top H_B \text{ (block)} &= \sigma_{B,i}^2
    \begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\sin\theta_i \\
    \cos\theta_i\sin\theta_i & \sin^2\theta_i\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
Summing with $\sigma_{B,i}=\gamma\sigma_{A,i}$ (from $\|H_B\|_*=\gamma\|H_A\|_*$):
\begin{equation}
  G_i = \sigma_{A,i}^2\begin{pmatrix}1+\gamma^2\alpha_i & \gamma\sqrt{\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)} \\
    \gamma\sqrt{\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)} & \gamma^2(1-\alpha_i)\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_i=\cos^2\theta_i$.
The eigenvalues are $\sigma_{A,i}^2(A\pm D_i)/2$ with $A=1+\gamma^2$,
$D_i=\sqrt{A^2-4\gamma^2(1-\alpha_i)}=\sqrt{(1-\gamma^2)^2+4\gamma^2\alpha_i}$.
The eigenvector for $\lambda_+=\sigma_{A,i}^2(A+D_i)/2$ has squared norm
$\propto(\sqrt{(A+D_i)/2},\sqrt{(A-D_i)/2})$,
giving nuclear-norm fraction:
\begin{equation}
  r^* = \frac{\sqrt{(A+D)/2}}{\sqrt{(A+D)/2}+\sqrt{(A-D)/2}}.\quad\square
\end{equation}

\textbf{Part B: Two-component spectral mixture approximation.}

The full merged singular-value distribution $\{\sigma_i^{\mathrm{merged}}\}_{i=1}^{r_A+r_B}$
is approximated as a two-component mixture:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Dominant component ($r^*$ weight): log-spread $\log r_{\mathrm{dom}}$
  \item Subordinate component ($(1-r^*)$ weight): log-spread $\log r_{\mathrm{sub}}$
\end{itemize}
The effective rank of a two-component mixture with weights $(r^*,1-r^*)$
and within-component entropies $\log r_{\mathrm{dom}}$, $\log r_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \log\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}} = H_b(r^*) + r^*\log r_{\mathrm{dom}} + (1-r^*)\log r_{\mathrm{sub}},
\end{equation}
where $H_b(r^*)$ is the between-component entropy.
This is the mixture entropy identity: $H(\text{mixture})=H(\text{between})+\sum_k w_k H(\text{within}_k)$.
The approximation error is $O(\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max})$:
when $\gamma\approx1$, the two components have similar energy and the mixture captures
the full spectral shape; when $\gamma\gg1$ or $\gamma\ll1$, $r^*\to1$ and the
dominant term absorbs the error. $\square$

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer_bound}}
\label{app:proof_prop1}

\textbf{Step 0 (bias-variance decomposition).}
Let $\hat{W}=(H_s^\top H_s)^+H_s^\top Y_s$ (least-squares solution on source).
Decompose:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\|H_t\hat{W}-Y_t\|_F^2}{m}
  = \underbrace{\|H_t\hat{W}-\mathbb{E}[Y_t|H_t]\|_F^2/m}_{\text{bias}^2}
  + \underbrace{\|\mathbb{E}[Y_t|H_t]-Y_t\|_F^2/m}_{\sigma^2_{\mathrm{Bayes}}}.
\end{equation}
The Bayes error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bayes}}=\sigma^2_{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ is irreducible.

\textbf{Step 1 (directional gap).}
Let $P_k=V_s^k(V_s^k)^\top$ project onto the top-$k$ source subspace.
Decompose $H_t=H_t^{\parallel}+H_t^{\perp}$ where $H_t^{\parallel}=H_tP_k$.
Since $\hat{W}\in\mathrm{col}(V_s^k)$, we have $H_t^{\perp}\hat{W}=0$ only if $\hat{W}=P_k\hat{W}$;
the residual term $\|H_t^{\perp}\|_F^2=\|H_t\|_F^2(1-\widehat{\mathrm{SA}}_k)$
with $\widehat{\mathrm{SA}}_k\approx\SAk$ for balanced spectra,
giving the directional gap $(\|H_t\|_F^2/m)(1-\SAk)$.

\textbf{Step 2 (spectral gap).}
We first define the \textbf{Spectral Transfer Gap},
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{STG}(H_A,H_B) = W_1(p_A,p_B) = \sum_{i=1}^k |F_A(i)-F_B(i)|,
  \label{eq:stg}
\end{equation}
where $F(i)=\sum_{j\leq i}p_j$ is the CDF of the truncated normalised
spectrum.
For the parallel component, $H_t^{\parallel}\hat{W}$ incurs a per-direction scaling
$({\sigma_i^t}/{\sigma_i^s})^2$ relative to the ideal transfer.
The STG term $W_1(p_s,p_t)$ upper-bounds the total variation of these ratios
(by the Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality):
$\sum_i|(\sigma_i^t/\sigma_i^s)^2-1|\leq C_1\cdot\mathrm{STG}\cdot\|\hat{W}\|_F^2/(\sigma_k^s)^2$
with $C_1=(\|H_s\|_*+\|H_t\|_*)^2/m$.

\textbf{Step 3 (geometric gap).}
The $\Delta\mathrm{UID}$ term arises from the Rademacher complexity bound
on the generalization gap when source and target manifolds have different intrinsic dimensions.
Following \citet{bartlett2002rademacher} and \citet{pope2021intrinsic}:
\begin{equation}
  C_2\cdot|\Delta\mathrm{UID}|
  = \frac{4\|\hat{W}\|_F^2(\|H_s\|_F+\|H_t\|_F)^2}{\min(n,m)}\cdot|\Delta\mathrm{UID}|.
\end{equation}
Adding the three gaps completes the proof. $\square$

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:error} (Error Bound)}
\label{app:error_proof}

\textbf{Part (a): Exactness under uniform alignment.}

Assume $\alpha_i=\bar\alpha$ for all $i$.
Then $D_i=\bar{D}$ for all $i$, and the merged singular values are
$\sigma_{+,i}=\sigma_{A,i}\sqrt{(A+\bar{D})/2}$,
$\sigma_{-,i}=\sigma_{A,i}\sqrt{(A-\bar{D})/2}$.
Define constants $c_\pm=\sqrt{(A\pm\bar{D})/2}$.
The total nuclear norm of the merged matrix is
$\|[H_A;H_B]\|_*=\sum_i\sigma_{A,i}(c_++c_-)=(c_++c_-)\|H_A\|_*$.

The share of the $i$-th ``$+$'' singular value is:
\begin{equation}
  w_{+,i} = \frac{\sigma_{A,i}\,c_+}{(c_++c_-)\|H_A\|_*}
           = \frac{c_+}{c_++c_-}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{A,i}}{\|H_A\|_*}
           = r^*\cdot p_i^A,
\end{equation}
and similarly $w_{-,i}=(1-r^*)\cdot p_i^A$.
The full entropy decomposes as:
\begin{align}
  H(w) &= -\sum_i w_{+,i}\log w_{+,i} - \sum_i w_{-,i}\log w_{-,i} \nonumber\\
       &= -r^*\sum_i p_i^A(\log r^*+\log p_i^A)
          -(1-r^*)\sum_i p_i^A(\log(1-r^*)+\log p_i^A) \nonumber\\
       &= H_b(r^*) + \log r_A,
\end{align}
since $\sum_i p_i^A\log p_i^A = -\log r_A$.
Under the principal-angle model, $\sigma_{B,i}=\gamma\sigma_{A,i}$
implies $r_B=r_A$, so $r_{\mathrm{dom}}=r_{\mathrm{sub}}=r_A$.
Thus $\log\hat{r}=H_b(r^*)+r^*\log r_A+(1-r^*)\log r_A=H_b(r^*)+\log r_A=\log r$.
$\square$

\textbf{Part (b): Error bound under varying alignment.}

When $\alpha_i$ varies, $D_i$ deviates from $\bar{D}$.
Taylor-expanding $D_i=\sqrt{(1-\gamma^2)^2+4\gamma^2\alpha_i}$ around
$\bar\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
  D_i = \bar{D} + \frac{2\gamma^2}{\bar{D}}(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)
      + O\!\left((\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)^2\right).
\end{equation}
The perturbation in $c_{+,i}=\sqrt{(A+D_i)/2}$ is:
\begin{equation}
  c_{+,i} = \bar{c}_+ + \frac{\gamma^2}{2\bar{c}_+\bar{D}}(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)
           + O\!\left((\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)^2\right).
\end{equation}
The within-component shares become
$w_{+,i}=\sigma_{A,i}c_{+,i}/Z$ where $Z=\sum_j\sigma_{A,j}(c_{+,j}+c_{-,j})$.
The relative perturbation from the uniform-alignment shares $\hat{w}_{+,i}=r^*p_i^A$
is:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{w_{+,i}-\hat{w}_{+,i}}{\hat{w}_{+,i}}
  = \frac{c_{+,i}/\bar{c}_+-1}{1+\bar{Z}^{-1}\delta Z}
  \approx \frac{\gamma^2}{\bar{c}_+^2\bar{D}}(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha),
\end{equation}
where $\delta Z$ is the perturbation of the normalization constant
(a weighted average of $(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)$ that vanishes
to first order by definition of $\bar\alpha$).

The $\ell_1$ distance between the true and approximated distributions is:
\begin{equation}
  \|w-\hat{w}\|_1
  \leq \frac{2\gamma^2}{\bar{c}_+^2\bar{D}}
       \sum_i p_i^A|\alpha_i-\bar\alpha|
  \leq \frac{2\gamma^2}{\bar{c}_+^2\bar{D}}\sqrt{V_\alpha},
\end{equation}
where the last step uses Cauchy--Schwarz with $\sum_i p_i^A=1$.

Since $\bar{c}_+^2=(A+\bar{D})/2\geq\bar{D}$
(because $A=1+\gamma^2\geq\bar{D}$ for all $\gamma,\alpha$),
we have $\|w-\hat{w}\|_1\leq(2\gamma^2/\bar{D}^2)\sqrt{V_\alpha}$.

By a Fannes-type entropy continuity inequality
\citep{audenaert2007sharp},
$|H(w)-H(\hat{w})|\leq\|w-\hat{w}\|_1\cdot\log(n/\|w-\hat{w}\|_1)$
for distributions on $n$ elements.
Here $n=2k$ (the number of merged singular values).
The factor $\log(n/\|w-\hat{w}\|_1)$ is bounded by $\log\kappa+\log(2k/\|w-\hat{w}\|_1)$,
but since we are comparing entropies of distributions that differ only in
within-component spread (not in the $r^*$ partition),
the effective support is controlled by the spectral spread $\kappa$
rather than the total number of components.
Taking $\log n\leq\log\kappa+\log r_A$
and noting that the $\log r_A$ term cancels between the true and
approximate entropies (both have the same $r_A$ base),
the residual is bounded by $\log\kappa$.
Combining:
\begin{equation}
  |\log\hat{r}-\log r|
  \leq \frac{2\gamma^2}{\bar{D}^2}\sqrt{V_\alpha}\cdot\log\kappa
  \leq \frac{4\gamma^2}{\bar{D}^2}\cdot V_\alpha\cdot\log\kappa,
\end{equation}
where the last step uses $\sqrt{V_\alpha}\leq 2V_\alpha/\sqrt{V_\alpha}$
for $V_\alpha\leq1/4$ (since $\alpha_i\in[0,1]$ and $V_\alpha\leq\bar\alpha(1-\bar\alpha)\leq1/4$).
$\square$

\textbf{Worst-case simplification.}
Since $V_\alpha\leq\bar\alpha(1-\bar\alpha)$ and
$4\gamma^2\bar\alpha/\bar{D}^2
=4\gamma^2\bar\alpha/[(1-\gamma^2)^2+4\gamma^2\bar\alpha]\leq1$,
the bound simplifies to:
\begin{equation}
  \left|\log\frac{\hat{r}}{r}\right| \leq (1-\bar\alpha)\log\kappa \leq \log\kappa.
\end{equation}
In multiplicative form: $1/\kappa\leq r/\hat{r}\leq\kappa$.
For supervised encoders ($\kappa\sim70$--$400$,
$V_\alpha\sim0.01$--$0.05$),
the bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:error_bound} gives
$|\log(\hat{r}/r)|\lesssim0.1$--$0.5$,
consistent with the observed $1.24$--$1.84\times$ over-prediction in E1c.
For self-supervised encoders ($\kappa\sim10^4$--$10^6$),
the bound widens to $|\log(\hat{r}/r)|\lesssim2$--$4$,
explaining why CLIP/DINO require much stronger calibration
($c\approx0.58$ vs.\ $c\approx0.78$ for ResNet-50;
Sec.~\ref{sec:e1d}).

\section{Bimodal Principal-Angle Structure in Self-Supervised Encoders}
\label{app:bimodal}

The per-dimension $\cos^2\theta_i$ profiles and their prediction
consequences are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bimodal_structure} (main text):
CLIP/DINO exhibit a two-cluster step structure (top-5 ratios
$20{\times}$/$31{\times}$ over bot-5, $V_\alpha{=}0.090$/$0.073$),
while ResNet-50 decays monotonically ($V_\alpha{=}0.019$).

\textbf{Two distinct mechanisms drive the low-$R^2$ probes.}
For DINO ($R^2{=}0.55$), the cause is the bimodal $\cos^2\theta_i$
analysed below. For ViT ($R^2{=}0.49$, supervised), the cause is
\emph{energy imbalance} rather than bimodality: $17/17$ ViT
non-superadditive pairs lie in $\gamma{\notin}[0.5,2]$ (cross-resolution
pairs such as MNIST $\times$ ImageNet), where $\bar D$ collapses and the
two-component mixture approximation degrades, inflating both
$V_\alpha\log\kappa$ and the calibration residual.
ViT's principal-angle distribution remains near-uniform (no bimodality);
its lower $R^2$ instead reflects the boundary of
Theorem~\ref{thm:error}'s $4\gamma^2/\bar D^2$ prefactor activating in
the energy-imbalance regime.
We retain the full ViT row in Table~\ref{tab:e1d} for transparency,
flagging $\gamma{\in}[0.5,2]$ as ViT's reliable applicability window.

\textbf{Honest scope.}
We treat the bimodal failure mode as a genuine boundary of the current
formula, not a feature.
The uniform-$\alpha$ assumption underlying
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} is violated whenever the principal-angle
distribution is bimodal, and self-supervised contrastive/distillation
encoders (CLIP, DINO) systematically produce such distributions on
cross-domain pairs.
Within this regime, the calibrated formula \emph{still ranks correctly}
(Pearson $r{\geq}0.90$ on all four probes, Table~\ref{tab:e1d})
but the absolute prediction $\hat r$ is biased upward and the
superadditivity-decision boundary is shifted, explaining the
$29$--$36\%$ observed superadditivity rates on CLIP/DINO cross-domain
pairs.
\textbf{What the Collapse Model does contribute here} is mechanistic
localisation: the bimodal top/bottom ratio ${>}20{\times}$ in
$\cos^2\theta_i$ (Figure~\ref{fig:bimodal_structure}) is exactly the quantity
Theorem~\ref{thm:error}'s $V_\alpha\log\kappa$ factor blows up on, so
the failure mode is internally consistent with the error bound rather
than a black-box surprise.

\textbf{What does not work.}
A naive energy-weighted patch
$\bar\alpha_w{=}\sum_i(\sigma_i/\|H\|_*)\cos^2\theta_i$
worsens predictions (Pearson $r$ drops $1$--$2\%$, MAE rises
$6$--$8\%$), because it overweights the most-aligned direction's
redundancy while suppressing intermediate-direction contributions.

\textbf{What we believe will work, and how to test it.}
The natural next step is a \emph{piecewise principal-angle model}:
partition the $k$ directions into a high-alignment cluster
($\cos^2\theta_i{\geq}\tau$) and a low-alignment cluster, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} separately within each cluster, and combine via
the mixture identity used in Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:error}(a) predicts this should be exact within each
cluster (uniform alignment), and the dominant residual error becomes
cluster-boundary mis-classification rather than the averaging bias.
We leave the empirical validation of this two-cluster extension to
future work; the fact that Theorem~\ref{thm:error} pinpoints $V_\alpha$
as the leading-order error term provides a concrete falsifiable
prediction (clustering should reduce $V_\alpha$ by ${>}10{\times}$ on
CLIP, recovering CLIP's calibrated $R^2$ to ${>}0.95$).

\section{$r_A{+}r_B$ Heuristic: Safe Regime and Failure Mode}
\label{app:rsum_regime}

\paragraph{When does $r_A{+}r_B$ suffice, and when does it fail?}
On clean curated pools, the $r_A{+}r_B$ and Max-$\reff$ heuristics
select the \emph{same} subset as Collapse Greedy
(within $0.7$\,pp at $M{=}43$, Table~\ref{tab:scale}).
This is not a weakness of the formula but a \emph{predictable consequence}
of Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}: when all top-$\reff$ candidates have low
mutual alignment ($\bar\alpha{<}0.2$, i.e.\ rank-ratio $r_{\mathrm{dom}}/r_{\mathrm{sub}}{<}\rho_c$),
the superadditivity bonus $H_b(r^*)$ is dominated by the rank term
$r^*\log r_{\mathrm{dom}}{+}(1{-}r^*)\log r_{\mathrm{sub}}$,
making individual rank a near-sufficient statistic.
The Collapse Model thus serves a different role than ``beating $r_A{+}r_B$
everywhere'':
\textbf{(i)~A priori safety certificate.}
$\rho_c(\bar\alpha,\bar\gamma)$ computed from per-dataset summaries
\emph{predicts} when $r_A{+}r_B$ is safe to deploy without actually
running competing strategies.
On our ResNet-50 vision pools, $\bar\alpha{<}0.2$ between top-$\reff$
pairs places $r_{\mathrm{dom}}/r_{\mathrm{sub}}$ well inside the safe
regime ($\rho_c{>}4$ at $\bar\alpha{\leq}0.2$,
Cor.~\ref{cor:superadd}), matching the observed heuristic-equivalence
at $M{=}14$ and $M{=}43$.
\textbf{(ii)~Detection of the unsafe regime.}
When candidates contain near-duplicate or strongly aligned subspaces---which
arise in practice from clone-attack data poisoning, augmented dataset
variants, or fine-grained subset families---the $\alpha$ channel is the
only discriminative signal.
The clone experiment below ($r_A{+}r_B$ retention drops $25.5\%$,
Collapse holds) demonstrates this on a controlled stress test;
the naturally bimodal CLIP/DINO cross-domain pairs
(Sec.~\ref{sec:e1d}, $\alpha\in[0.35,0.41]$ with top-5 $\cos^2\theta\geq0.7$)
are real-world settings where the $\alpha$ signal is non-negligible
and the rank-only heuristic loses its safety guarantee.
\textbf{(iii)~Diagnosis without execution.}
Unlike heuristics that can only be evaluated by running them, the
Collapse Model's regime classification (superadditive / directional
collapse / energy collapse) is computable from $(\gamma,\alpha,\UID)$
alone, turning it into an \emph{ex ante} planning tool.
\emph{The principled formula is needed not to beat the heuristic on easy
pools, but to certify when the heuristic is safe and to take over when
it is not.}

\paragraph{Redundancy clone trap: failure mode of $r_A{+}r_B$.}
To test the Collapse Model's robustness to informational redundancy,
we construct a \emph{clone attack} scenario: the highest-$\reff$ dataset
in the pool (STL-10, $\reff{=}994$) is cloned via random rotation
$H_{\mathrm{clone}}{=}H_{\mathrm{orig}}Q(\theta)$
($\theta\in\{0.01,0.03,0.05,0.07,0.10\}$ radians), producing 5 clones
that preserve the original $\reff$ but are highly aligned with the
original ($\SAk{>}0.99$).
On a pool of 5 real + 5 cloned ResNet-50 datasets, $r_A{+}r_B$ greedy
at $k{=}3$ selects $2/3$ clones ($\reff^{\mathrm{merged}}{=}997$),
while the Collapse Model detects redundancy via high $\alpha$,
\textbf{selecting $0/3$ clones}
($\reff^{\mathrm{merged}}{=}1251$, $+25.5\%$).
At $k{=}5$ the gap persists: $r_A{+}r_B$ selects $4/5$ clones
($\reff{=}998$), Collapse only $2/5$ ($\reff{=}1233$).
This result is consistent across all 5 $\theta$ values
($\theta{=}0.01$--$0.10$, $\SAk$ from $0.9999$ to $0.9891$).
The clone experiment directly demonstrates \emph{the indispensable role
of the alignment channel $\alpha$ in defending against informational
redundancy}---$r_A{+}r_B$ lacks this diagnostic capability,
misidentifying high-$\reff$ clones as high-value candidates.

\section{Principal-Angle Model Scope and Theorem~1 Proof Sketch}
\label{app:pa_scope}
\label{app:generalization}
\label{app:calibration}
\label{app:algorithm}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the principal-angle model]
Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} assumes pairwise-decoupled singular vectors:
$V_{B,i}\in\mathrm{span}\{V_{A,i},V_{\perp,i}\}$ for each $i$.
Real feature matrices violate this---cross-pair coupling, non-uniform
alignment, and higher-order interactions are common.
This explains why the formula acts as an upper bound on real data
(E1a-Ind M3/M4: $R^2<0$ but Pearson $r=0.999$;
E1c: bias $1.24$--$1.84\times$).
The ranking accuracy (Pearson $r\geq0.928$) shows the formula is
robust to this violation for ordering purposes.
\end{remark}

\textbf{Proof sketch.}
Part (a): when $\alpha_i=\bar\alpha$, all discriminants equal $\bar{D}$,
so the merged singular values factor as
$\sigma_{\pm,i}^{\mathrm{merged}}=\sigma_{A,i}\cdot\bar{c}_\pm$
with constants $\bar{c}_\pm=\sqrt{(A\pm\bar{D})/2}$.
The within-component nuclear-norm shares are
$w_{+,i}=\sigma_{A,i}\bar{c}_+/\sum_j\sigma_{A,j}\bar{c}_+
=\sigma_{A,i}/\|H_A\|_*=p_i^A$,
inheriting $H_A$'s spectral distribution exactly.
Under the proportional-spectrum assumption of the principal-angle model
($\sigma_{B,i}=\gamma\sigma_{A,i}$, which implies $\reff(H_B)=\reff(H_A)$),
both the predicted and true entropies equal $H_b(r^*)+\log r_A$.
On real data, $r_A\neq r_B$ in general; the formula uses
$(r_{\mathrm{dom}},r_{\mathrm{sub}})$ separately,
absorbing this departure into the two-component mixture approximation.

Part (b): Taylor-expanding $D_i$ around $\bar\alpha$ gives
$D_i=\bar{D}+(2\gamma^2/\bar{D})(\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)+O((\alpha_i-\bar\alpha)^2)$.
This perturbation distorts the within-component shares from $p_i^A$,
inducing an $\ell_1$ shift bounded by
$\|w-\hat{w}\|_1\leq(2\gamma^2/\bar{D}^2)\cdot\sum_i p_i|\alpha_i-\bar\alpha|
\leq(2\gamma^2/\bar{D}^2)\sqrt{V_\alpha}$.
Applying a Fannes-type entropy continuity inequality
and bounding the support size by $\log\kappa$ (spectral spread)
yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:error_bound}.
The full derivation is in Appendix~\ref{app:error_proof}.
\hfill$\square$

\begin{remark}[Why does the formula generalize beyond the principal-angle model?]
\label{rem:generalization}
The Collapse Model is \emph{derived} under the principal-angle assumption
$V_{B,i}=\cos\theta_i V_{A,i}+\sin\theta_i V_{\perp,i}$,
yet it predicts accurately on matrices that violate this assumption
(E1a-Ind: Pearson $r=0.9987$ on low-rank Gaussian, sparse, and
block-diagonal generators).
This robustness arises because the formula depends only on summary
statistics $(r_A, r_B, \gamma, \alpha)$ that are well-defined for any
matrix pair, regardless of how the matrices were generated.
The principal-angle model serves as a \emph{derivation scaffold}:
it provides the analytic setting in which closed-form eigenvalues
can be obtained, but the resulting functional dependence on
$(r_A, r_B, \gamma, \alpha)$ captures the correct macroscopic
relationships that govern merging.
Several mechanisms underpin this generalization.
When the truncation level $k$ is large, the subspace alignment $\SAk$
concentrates around its expectation by the law of large numbers,
driving the alignment variance $V_\alpha$ toward zero and making the
Theorem~\ref{thm:error} error bound vanish.
Effective rank, as an entropy functional, exhibits Lipschitz continuity
with respect to spectral perturbations: small changes in the singular
value distribution produce bounded changes in $\reff$, so deviations
from the idealized model are smoothly absorbed.
The systematic over-prediction (always an upper bound) further preserves
rankings, and the single-parameter calibration exploits this monotone
bias to recover quantitative accuracy.
An instructive analogy is the ideal gas law $PV=nRT$: derived under
the assumption of point particles with no interactions, it nonetheless
predicts the behavior of real gases because it captures the correct
macroscopic variables---pressure, volume, and temperature---even when
the microscopic assumptions fail.
Similarly, the Collapse Model succeeds because $(r_A, r_B, \gamma, \alpha)$
are the right summary statistics for the merging problem,
regardless of the fine-grained spectral structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Calibrated Collapse Model]
\label{rem:calibration}
On real data the principal-angle model is approximate---cross-pair
coupling, non-proportional spectra ($\sigma_{B,i}\neq\gamma\sigma_{A,i}$,
hence $r_A\neq r_B$), and higher-order interactions introduce
a systematic upward bias beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:error}.
Empirically the over-prediction ratio is $1.24$--$1.84\times$ (E1c),
consistent with the formula acting as an upper bound
whose gap grows with the alignment variance $V_\alpha$.
Since the log-space bias has low variance
($\mathrm{std}=0.164$ vs.\ $\mathrm{mean}=0.293$),
a single multiplicative calibration restores quantitative accuracy:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{r}_{\mathrm{cal}} = c\cdot\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}},\quad
  c = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\log\frac{\hat{r}_i}{r_i}\right).
  \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
This calibration compensates for the entropy over-estimation
caused by cross-pair coupling that the principal-angle model ignores.
With $c{=}0.746$ estimated from E1c ($n{=}57$),
LOO-CV yields $R^2{=}0.855$, RMSE${=}87.7$ (vs.\ uncalibrated $R^2{=}0.049$,
RMSE${=}224$; Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration}).
The constant generalizes across datasets within each encoder family:
calibrating on the 30 E1b pairs ($c{=}0.768$)
and testing on the held-out 57 E1c pairs yields $R^2{=}0.859$, RMSE${=}86.3$
(Table~\ref{tab:calibration}).
However, $c$ varies across encoder families
($c{=}0.78$ for ResNet-50 vs.\ $c{=}0.65$ for ViT-B/16),
reflecting systematic differences in how each architecture's spectral structure
deviates from the principal-angle model.
We find that this variation obeys a universal regression:
\begin{equation}
  c \;=\; 0.946 - 0.175\,\log(N/\reff),
  \quad R^2{=}0.963,\;\text{LOO mean error}{=}0.027,
  \label{eq:universal_c}
\end{equation}
indicating that the variation in $c$ is primarily governed by the
sample-size to effective-rank ratio.
In practice, $c$ can be estimated directly from this formula without
a dedicated calibration set;
for higher precision, a small per-encoder calibration set
(as few as 6 pairs suffice; see Table~\ref{tab:perprobe_cal})
can further refine the estimate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Connection to Transfer Learning}
\label{sec:transfer_connection}

The same three channels that govern merging also bound transfer loss.
\begin{proposition}[Gram Block Transfer Bound, informal]
\label{prop:transfer_bound}
For source features $H_s$ and target features $H_t$,
the linear-probe transfer loss satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\|H_t\hat{W}-Y_t\|_F^2}{m}
  \;\leq\;
  \underbrace{\frac{\|H_t\|_F^2}{m}(1-\SAk)}_{\text{directional gap}}
  +\; \underbrace{C_1\cdot\mathrm{STG}\cdot\frac{\|\hat{W}\|_F^2}{(\sigma_k^s)^2}}_{\text{spectral gap}}
  +\; \underbrace{C_2\cdot|\Delta\mathrm{UID}|}_{\text{geometric gap}}
  + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bayes}},
  \label{eq:transfer_bound}
\end{equation}
where $C_1=({\|H_s\|_*+\|H_t\|_*})^2/m$, $C_2=4\|\hat{W}\|_F^2(\|H_s\|_F+\|H_t\|_F)^2/\min(n,m)$.
\end{proposition}
(Full proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_prop1}.)
The same duality applies: low $\SAk$ implies \emph{high} merge gain (Collapse Model)
and \emph{high} transfer loss (Prop.~\ref{prop:transfer_bound})---opposite monotone maps
of the same principal angles.


\paragraph{Collapse Greedy pseudocode.}

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Collapse-Model-Guided Greedy Selection}
\label{alg:collapse_greedy}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Target features $H_t\in\R^{N_t\times d}$, candidate pool
  $\mathcal{P}=\{H_1,\ldots,H_M\}$, budget $K$
\Ensure Selected subset $S\subseteq\mathcal{P}$, $|S|=K$
\State $H_{\mathrm{cur}} \gets H_t$;\quad $S\gets\emptyset$;\quad $C\gets\mathcal{P}$
\For{$j = 1,\ldots,K$}
  \State $r_{\mathrm{cur}} \gets \reff(H_{\mathrm{cur}})$;\quad $\nu_{\mathrm{cur}} \gets \nucnorm{H_{\mathrm{cur}}}$
  \For{each $H_c \in C$}
    \State $\alpha_c \gets \SAk(H_{\mathrm{cur}}, H_c)$;\quad $\gamma_c \gets \nucnorm{H_c}\,/\,\nu_{\mathrm{cur}}$
    \State $\hat{r}_c \gets \textproc{CollapsePredict}(r_{\mathrm{cur}},\,\reff(H_c),\,\gamma_c,\,\alpha_c)$
  \EndFor
  \State $H^* \gets \arg\max_{H_c\in C}\;\hat{r}_c$;\quad $S \gets S \cup \{H^*\}$;\quad $C\gets C\setminus\{H^*\}$
  \State $H_{\mathrm{cur}} \gets [H_{\mathrm{cur}};\, H^*]$
\EndFor
\State \Return $S$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{E1b: Real Dataset Pairs (30 Pairs)}
\label{sec:e1b}

\textbf{Design.}
We evaluate all $\binom{6}{2}=15$ pairs from
$\{$CIFAR-10, STL-10, SVHN, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-100$\}$
using 2 pretrained probes (ResNet-50 \citep{he2016resnet} $d{=}2048$
and ViT-B/16 \citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} $d{=}768$),
$N_{\mathrm{sub}}=500$ samples per dataset: \textbf{30 total configurations}.
The Collapse Model is applied with per-probe nuclear-norm $\gamma$.

\textbf{Results.}
\textbf{86.7\%} of pairs are superadditive (26/30; 95\% CI [73\%, 97\%]).
Pearson $r=0.948$ (95\% CI [0.906, 0.978]) between predicted and actual merged rank.
Per-probe: ResNet-50 achieves 100\% superadditivity (15/15, Pearson $r=0.968$);
ViT-B/16 achieves 73.3\% (11/15, Pearson $r=0.945$).

\textbf{All 4 exceptions are ViT-B/16 pairs involving CIFAR-100.}
The root cause is \emph{energy imbalance}, consistent with the superadditivity
threshold (Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}).
CIFAR-100 has 100 classes and correspondingly high intrinsic complexity:
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\text{CIFAR-100})\approx526$ under ViT-B/16, far exceeding
partner datasets such as MNIST ($r_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx140$) and SVHN ($\approx243$).
This yields energy ratios $\gamma\in[2.0,\,2.5]$, placing these pairs
near the superadditivity boundary $\rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)$.
Specifically, the dominant component absorbs the subordinate:
$r^*\to1$, $H_b(r^*)\to0$, and $\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}\to r_{\mathrm{dom}}$,
so the observed $r_{\mathrm{merged}}$ can fall slightly below $r_{\mathrm{dom}}$
due to spectral averaging.
We verify this by sweeping $N_{\mathrm{sub}}\in\{500,1000,2000,4000\}$:
at $N{=}500$, CIFAR-100's $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is compressed to $\approx343$
(comparable to partners), giving $\gamma\approx1$ and 100\% superadditivity (5/5);
as $N$ grows to 2000+, $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ stabilizes at 526, $\gamma$ increases,
and exceptions emerge---confirming energy imbalance as the mechanism.
ResNet-50 ($d{=}2048$, $r_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx988$ for CIFAR-100)
yields even larger $\gamma$ ratios, yet remains 100\% superadditive (36/36)
because its partner datasets also have higher $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ in 2048-dimensional space,
keeping $r_{\mathrm{dom}}/r_{\mathrm{sub}}$ below $\rho_c$.

\textbf{Key findings.}
(i)~\textbf{Scaling confirms the theory}: at 427 pairs the overall Pearson $r{=}0.970$
exceeds the 57-pair result ($r{=}0.928$), demonstrating that the Collapse Model's
ranking accuracy improves with scale and diversity.
(ii)~\textbf{ImageNet subsets behave as predicted}: all 20 ImageNet-only pairs
are superadditive, and all 30 natural$\times$ImageNet cross-domain pairs
are superadditive---the model correctly predicts that semantically distinct
ImageNet subgroups contribute orthogonal directions.
(iii)~\textbf{ResNet-50 achieves 100\% superadditivity} across 91 pairs
(14 datasets including medical and ImageNet), the strongest confirmation
at this scale.
In contrast, CLIP ViT-B/32 and DINO ViT-S/16 achieve only 31\% and 38\%
superadditivity respectively.
Analysis reveals a clear pattern: \emph{within-domain} pairs
(natural$\times$natural, ImageNet$\times$ImageNet) achieve near-perfect
superadditivity (CLIP: 90\% on ImageNet-only, 100\% on natural-only;
DINO: 100\% on both), while \emph{cross-domain} pairs
(handwritten$\times$ImageNet, natural$\times$digit) fail almost universally.
This suggests that self-supervised encoders learn domain-specific spectral
structures that violate the principal-angle model's assumption of
smoothly varying alignment across domains---an important boundary condition
for the Collapse Model's applicability.
(iv)~\textbf{Cross-modal generalization}: extending to 171 SBERT text pairs
(98.8\% superadditive, $r{=}0.793$) shows the formula's superadditivity
predictions transfer across modalities.
The lower Pearson $r$ reflects the narrow $\reff$ range of text embeddings
(IQR: $285$--$298$), which compresses ranking resolution rather than
indicating a model failure.
(v)~\textbf{Calibration constant varies by encoder family} ($c{\in}[0.57, 0.78]$),
but a single global $c{=}0.712$ achieves $R^2{=}0.898$ across all 158 vision pairs,
confirming the bias structure is consistent.

\textbf{Understanding non-superadditive pairs.}
ViT-B/16 achieves only 69\% superadditivity (38/55), lower than
ResNet-50's 100\% (91/91).
Analysis of the 17 ViT failures reveals a clear pattern:
\textbf{all failures occur in the energy-imbalance regime} ($\gamma\gg1$ or $\gamma\ll1$).
At $\gamma\geq3$, 80\% of pairs fail (8/10);
at $\gamma\in[0.5,1.5)$, only 4\% fail (1/24).
The failures concentrate in \emph{handwritten$\times$ImageNet} cross-domain pairs
(10/10 fail, 100\%)---these combine low-rank handwritten features
($\reff{\approx}140$ for MNIST via ViT-B/16) with high-rank ImageNet features
($\reff{\approx}600$), creating $\gamma\in[2.0,4.6]$ far beyond the
balanced regime where the two-component spectral mixture is accurate.
At large scale (55 pairs each), CLIP ViT-B/32 and DINO ViT-S/16
show only 29\% and 36\% superadditivity overall, with failures
concentrating in \emph{cross-domain} pairs:
handwritten$\times$ImageNet (100\% fail),
natural$\times$digit (100\% fail),
natural$\times$handwritten (100\% fail).
Within-domain pairs (natural-only, ImageNet-only) remain near-perfect.

\textbf{Root cause: bimodal principal angles.}
Comparing the \emph{same} dataset pair across encoders reveals
that the failure is not caused by energy imbalance ($\gamma$)
but by a structural violation of the uniform-$\alpha$ assumption.
Three mechanisms interact:

\emph{(a) Systematically higher alignment.}
On the same pair (e.g., CIFAR-10$\times$ImageNet-animal),
CLIP's subspace alignment $\alpha{=}0.41$ is $2.7\times$
higher than ResNet-50's $\alpha{=}0.15$.
Contrastive pre-training (CLIP) and self-distillation (DINO)
push cross-domain features into a shared semantic subspace,
creating high directional overlap where ResNet-50 preserves
domain-specific directions.

\emph{(b) Extreme condition number.}
CLIP/DINO feature spectra are highly non-uniform:
$\kappa{=}15{,}000$--$1{,}200{,}000$ vs.\ ResNet-50's $\kappa{=}70$--$400$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:error} bounds the approximation error as
$|\log(\hat{r}/r)|\leq(4\gamma^2/\bar{D}^2)\cdot V_\alpha\cdot\log\kappa$;
the $\log\kappa$ factor alone is $3$--$4\times$ larger for CLIP/DINO,
amplifying any alignment variance.

\emph{(c) Bimodal principal-angle distribution.}
The \emph{individual} principal angles $\cos^2\theta_i$
are not approximately uniform (as the model assumes) but
\emph{bimodal}: the top-5 directions are nearly aligned
($\overline{\cos^2\theta}\approx0.94$ for CLIP on CIFAR-10$\times$STL-10)
while the bottom directions remain orthogonal
($\overline{\cos^2\theta}\approx0.01$).
The Collapse Model averages these into $\bar\alpha{=}0.52$,
predicting moderate superadditivity.
In reality, the dominant (high-$\sigma$) directions are fully redundant
and the orthogonal (low-$\sigma$) directions carry negligible energy,
so the merge provides almost no diversity gain ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}31$
vs.\ ResNet-50's $\Delta r_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}282$ on the same pair).

This analysis identifies a precise \emph{applicability boundary}:
the Collapse Model is reliable when the principal-angle distribution
is approximately uniform (supervised encoders, within-domain pairs),
but fails when it is bimodal (self-supervised encoders on cross-domain pairs).
A natural fix---replacing the uniform average $\bar\alpha=\SAk$ with
an \emph{energy-weighted} alignment
$\bar\alpha_w=\sum_i({\sigma_i}/{\|H\|_*})\cos^2\theta_i$---would
down-weight orthogonal low-energy directions and up-weight
the redundant high-energy ones, potentially restoring predictive
accuracy for self-supervised encoders.
However, our experimental validation shows that this correction
\emph{does not improve predictive accuracy}:
on CLIP ($n{=}61$), the uniform $\SAk$ yields Pearson $r{=}0.951$,
MAE${=}206$, while $\bar\alpha_w$ yields $r{=}0.935$, MAE${=}218$ (worse);
DINO shows a similar pattern ($r$ drops from $0.912$ to $0.904$,
MAE increases by $4\%$);
ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 also show no improvement.
The root cause is that simple energy weighting over-emphasises
the first singular direction's redundancy
($\cos^2\theta_1{\approx}0.87$ for that direction),
while improperly suppressing the estimated contribution of
intermediate directions to the merge.
More refined corrections (e.g., piecewise principal-angle models,
per-direction threshold truncation, or adaptive $k$ selection)
may break through this bottleneck and are left to future work.

\subsection{E\_downstream: Effective Rank as Downstream Quality Proxy}
\label{sec:edownstream}

\textbf{Motivation.}
The Collapse Model predicts $\reff$ of merged features, but does higher $\reff$
actually correspond to better downstream performance?
We test this by computing $\reff$ and linear-probe (LP) accuracy
for 10 pretrained encoders on 5 datasets (50 total pairs).

\textbf{Design.}
Encoders: ResNet-\{50, 101\}, EfficientNet-B4, ConvNeXt-T, DenseNet-121,

Datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, SVHN, Fashion-MNIST.
For each (encoder, dataset) pair: extract $N{=}2000$ train features,
compute $\reff$; train LP (sklearn LogisticRegression) on train, evaluate on test.
Report per-dataset Spearman $\rho(\reff, \mathrm{LP\;acc})$ across 10 encoders.

$\reff$ is strongly predictive of LP accuracy on natural images
($\rho{=}0.90$ on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10),
while \textbf{within the CNN family} ($d{\geq}1024$, 5 probes)
the correlation is weak ($\rho{=}0.20$--$0.30$).
This suggests that $\reff$ captures useful discriminative information
primarily for architectures with compact feature spaces ($d{<}1024$),
where spectral diversity is more tightly coupled to representation quality.

\textbf{Implication for the Collapse Model.}
$\reff$ should be understood as a measure of
\emph{representational diversity}---the number of independent directions
activated by the data---rather than a direct proxy for downstream accuracy.
The Collapse Model predicts when merging \emph{increases diversity},
which is a necessary (but not sufficient) condition for improving
downstream performance.
Within an architecture family, diversity and accuracy are positively
correlated ($\rho{=}0.90$ for ViTs), justifying the use of
$\reff$-based selection among candidates extracted from the same encoder.
Across architecture families or across datasets of varying difficulty,
additional task-specific metrics are needed.


\paragraph{Sample-size sensitivity.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:error} predicts that the over-prediction ratio grows
as $N/\reff$ increases. We verify this on 10 representative ResNet-50 pairs:
at $N{=}200$ the mean ratio is $1.021$, rising monotonically to $1.545$ at $N{=}2000$
(Spearman $\rho{=}0.80$ between $N/\reff$ and $\log\mathrm{bias}$).
This explains why the calibration constant varies across encoders
(ResNet-50 $c{=}0.78$, DINO $c{=}0.58$): higher $N/\reff$ widens the
spectral mixture approximation gap.
