\begin{abstract}
Given $M$ frozen-representation data pools, which candidates can be
shortlisted without labels before expensive evaluation?  We separate exact
spectral claims from empirical screening.  For fixed, label-independent
preprocessing and declared source weights, the merged spectrum is exactly
determined by a sum of positive-semidefinite Gram operators.  Under explicit
block-compatible singular directions this sum has an exact local
$2{\times}2$ decomposition; a constructive counterexample shows that
marginal spectra and principal angles---and hence the earlier four-scalar
summary---do not determine merged effective rank in general.  Our primary
Stage-1 method is a composable rank-$L$ PSD Gram sketch.  Transmitted tail
statistics give deterministic Fannes--Audenaert intervals for
log-effective-rank error and a sufficient certificate for one Full-Gram
greedy step.  Controlled sweeps across four encoders show that all 2,880
selected-prefix intervals cover the exact score, but none of 7,200 greedy
steps is certified for $L\leq50$, and DPP is at least as competitive.  In 28
held-out encoder-target units, even validation-oracle source-supervised
adapters trail frozen identity by $0.067$ (target-cluster 95\% CI
$[-0.098,-0.044]$); small shortlist regret is therefore not downstream
benefit.  We make no global-optimality, privacy, practical-compression, or
downstream-benefit claim.
Code is available at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/collapsemodel-1DB6}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern pretrained models increasingly rely on combining features from many
candidate datasets---multi-domain transfer learning, data curation for
self-supervised pretraining \citep{gadre2023datacomp},
and continual learning all face the question:
\emph{given $M$ candidate datasets, which $k{\ll}M$ subset preserves the
diversity of the full pool when their features are concatenated?}
We call this the \emph{label-free data-pool compression} problem:
unlike classical coreset selection \citep{mirzasoleiman2020coresets}
and recent \emph{sample-level} data curation methods
(DSIR~\citep{xie2023dsir}, DsDm~\citep{engstrom2024dsdm},
D$^2$ Pruning~\citep{maharana2023d2pruning}),
we seek compression at the \emph{dataset level}
(not the sample level), without access to labels or downstream tasks.
The criterion is representational diversity, formalized as the
effective rank $\reff(H)=\exp(-\sum_i p_i\log p_i)$ of the merged features
\citep{roy2007effective,garrido2023rankme}.
No prior work characterizes how dataset-level spectra and relative
subspace geometry jointly affect $\reff$ under feature concatenation,
forcing practitioners to rely on exhaustive search or heuristics
that scale poorly with $M$.

Prior work addresses related but distinct problems.
\emph{Spectral diversity metrics} such as RankMe~\citep{garrido2023rankme},
VNE~\citep{kim2023vne}, the Vendi Score~\citep{friedman2023vendi}, and its
generalized form~\citep{pasarkar2024cousins} quantify the diversity of a
single set via entropy of a singular-value or similarity-matrix spectrum;
they do not predict how two sets' diversity combines under concatenation.
\emph{Label-free coreset selection} (ELFS~\citep{shen2025elfs}, ZCore~\citep{griffin2024zcore}, DEFT-UCS~\citep{das2024deft}) selects a subset of \emph{samples} using geometry or proxy dynamics; it is complementary but orthogonal to our dataset-level formulation.
\emph{Pool-level curation} (DataComp~\citep{gadre2023datacomp}, DataComp-LM~\citep{li2024dclm}, DsDm~\citep{engstrom2024dsdm}, Quad~\citep{zhang2025quad}) uses filtering or model-aware heuristics without a closed-form diversity criterion.
\emph{Representation similarity measures} (SVCCA \citep{raghu2017svcca}, PWCCA \citep{morcos2018pwcca}, CKA \citep{kornblith2019similarity}, PARC \citep{bolya2021parc}) compare two representations but do not predict merged rank.
\emph{Transferability estimators} (LogME \citep{zhang2021logme}, H-score \citep{bao2019hscore}, TransRate \citep{huang2022transrate}, LEEP \citep{li2021nleep}, reassessed by \citet{ibrahim2022newer}) and PMI-based dataset valuation~\citep{zhang2024pmi} predict downstream accuracy but either require labels or do not model merging.
Task2Vec \citep{achille2019task2vec} avoids labels but requires probe fine-tuning.
Model-merging methods (Task Arithmetic~\citep{ilharco2023task}, TIES-Merging~\citep{yadav2023ties}, DARE~\citep{yu2024dare}) combine parameters, not features.
Intrinsic-dimensionality work \citep{pope2021intrinsic,ansuini2019intrinsic} characterizes individual matrices but not merged ones.
To our knowledge, no existing work combines an exact local spectral analysis
of $\reff([H_A;H_B])$ with a summary-statistic predictor for dataset-level selection.

We start from the exact identity $G_{A\cup B}=G_A+G_B$ and keep two
approximations conceptually separate.  The earlier four-summary predictor,
which we call \textbf{Collapse-4S}, compresses a paired-direction model into
$(r_A,r_B,\gamma,\alpha)$.  It is useful for mechanism diagnostics and as an
empirical baseline, but Proposition~\ref{prop:insufficient} proves that these
summaries are not sufficient in general.  The primary computational method,
\textbf{Collapse-Gram-$L$}, instead transmits a rank-$L$ PSD factor and
certified tail statistics for every source.  These summaries compose by
addition, retain spectral pairing within the retained subspace, and provide a
deterministic error interval (Theorem~\ref{thm:rank_l_bound}).

\textbf{Why summary-based screening?}
Exhaustive search requires $\binom{M}{k}$ merged decompositions and access to
all feature rows.  Collapse-Gram-$L$ uses $O(kM)$ candidate evaluations and
communicates $O(Ld)$ values plus three tail statistics per source.  It does
not require raw sample features at the coordinator, but this compression is
not a differential-privacy guarantee.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~\emph{Exact algebra and an information limit}: an unconditional Gram
representation, an exact block-compatible decomposition, and an explicit
counterexample to low-dimensional identifiability
(Propositions~\ref{prop:gram_general}--\ref{prop:insufficient}).
(2)~\emph{Certified compression}: a composable rank-$L$ PSD sketch with a
tail-dependent log-effective-rank interval and a sufficient certificate for
matching one step of Full-Gram greedy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rank_l_bound}, Corollary~\ref{cor:rank_l_choice}).
(3)~\emph{An audited two-stage protocol}: label-blind Stage-1 sampling,
declared equal-source weighting, target-isolated Stage-2 evaluation, matched
overlap diagnostics, source-independent controls, and provenance-checked
resume semantics (Sec.~\ref{sec:compression}).

\textbf{Empirical scope.}
The existing pairwise experiments evaluate Collapse-4S, not the new
rank-$L$ guarantee.  They show strong ordering correlation on the evaluated
pairs, but only a $0.003$ Spearman improvement over $r_A{+}r_B$ and
encoder-dependent calibration.  Historical pool experiments likewise show
that spectral methods often select similar clean-pool subsets.  We retain
these results as bounded evidence and report corrected rank-$L$, matched
overlap, and task-aware comparisons separately rather than treating the old
tables as validation of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank_l_bound}.

\section{Spectral Background}
\label{sec:background}

For a feature matrix $H\in\R^{N\times d}$ with compact SVD
$H=U\Sigma V^\top$, let
$\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_r>0)$.

\textbf{$\Sigma$ channel --- spectral energy.}
The \textbf{effective rank} summarizes spectral distribution:
\begin{equation}
  \reff(H) = \exp\!\left(-\sum_{i=1}^r p_i \log p_i\right),
  \quad p_i = \frac{\sigma_i}{\sum_j \sigma_j},
  \label{eq:reff}
\end{equation}
where $p_i$ is the singular-value share (not squared), so $r_{\mathrm{eff}}\in[1,r]$.
The nuclear norm $\|H\|_*=\sum_i\sigma_i$ is the normalizing mass.

\textbf{$V$ channel --- directional structure.}
Right singular vectors $V$ encode which directions are activated.
\begin{definition}[Subspace Alignment]
\label{def:sa}
Let $\hat{V}_A^k,\hat{V}_B^k\in\R^{d\times k}$ be the top-$k$ right singular
vectors of $H_A,H_B$.
The \textbf{Subspace Alignment} at level $k$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \SAk(H_A,H_B) = \frac{1}{k}\norm{\hat{V}_A^{k\top}\hat{V}_B^k}_F^2
  = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^k\cos^2\theta_i,
  \label{eq:sak}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_1\leq\cdots\leq\theta_k$ are the principal angles.
\end{definition}
$\SAk\in[0,1]$ summarizes top-subspace overlap, but discards the
principal-vector basis and its coupling to singular values.  This loss of
information is central to Proposition~\ref{prop:insufficient}.

\section{The Collapse Model}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $H_A\in\R^{N_A\times d}$ and $H_B\in\R^{N_B\times d}$ be L2-row-normalized feature matrices
with effective ranks $r_A=\reff(H_A)$, $r_B=\reff(H_B)$,
nuclear norms $\|H_A\|_*$, $\|H_B\|_*$, and subspace alignment $\alpha=\SAk(H_A,H_B)$.
Define:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma = \frac{\|H_B\|_*}{\|H_A\|_*} \in (0,\infty),
  \quad
  r_{\mathrm{dom}} = \begin{cases}r_A & \gamma\leq1 \\ r_B & \gamma>1\end{cases},
  \quad
  r_{\mathrm{sub}} = \begin{cases}r_B & \gamma\leq1 \\ r_A & \gamma>1\end{cases}.
\end{equation}
$\gamma$ is the \textbf{energy ratio} of the two matrices;
$r_{\mathrm{dom}}$ is the effective rank of the higher-energy matrix.

All source preprocessing is fixed before subset selection.  For source $j$
with $N_j$ rows, we declare a total source weight $\beta_j>0$ and use
$X_j=\sqrt{\beta_j/N_j}\,H_j$.  Our unequal-size default is $\beta_j=1$
(equal total mass per source); $\beta_j=N_j$ is the declared equal-per-sample
alternative.  Although effective rank is invariant to a common scale, it is
not invariant to changing relative source weights.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:theorem}

Our analysis separates three levels that must not be conflated:
an unconditional Gram identity, an exact paired-direction model under
explicit decoupling assumptions, and a low-dimensional summary predictor
for general spectra.

\begin{proposition}[Exact Gram Representation]
\label{prop:gram_general}
Let $H_A=U_A\Sigma_A V_A^\top$ and
$H_B=U_B\Sigma_B V_B^\top$ be compact SVDs, and complete $V_A$ to an
orthogonal basis $Q_A=[V_A\;V_{A,\perp}]$.  With
$C=Q_A^\top V_B$ and $\widetilde\Sigma_A^2$ denoting $\Sigma_A^2$
padded by zeros to dimension $d$, the merged Gram operator satisfies
\begin{equation}
 Q_A^\top(H_A^\top H_A+H_B^\top H_B)Q_A
 =\widetilde\Sigma_A^2+C\Sigma_B^2C^\top.
 \label{eq:general_gram}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the merged singular values are the square roots of the
eigenvalues of the right-hand side.
\end{proposition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:general_gram} is unconditional.  In general $C$ is
dense: a singular direction of one matrix can couple to several directions
of the other.  Individual spectra and an average principal-angle statistic
therefore do not determine the merged spectrum.

\begin{lemma}[Exact: 2$\times$2 Gram Block Eigenstructure]
\label{lem:gram}
Assume paired, decoupled directions: for each $i$,
$V_{B,i}=\cos\theta_i V_{A,i}+\sin\theta_i V_{\perp,i}$, where the
two-dimensional planes $\mathrm{span}\{V_{A,i},V_{\perp,i}\}$ are mutually
orthogonal.  Define the \emph{mode-wise} energy ratio
$\gamma_i=\sigma_{B,i}/\sigma_{A,i}$ and
$\alpha_i=\cos^2\theta_i$.  The $i$-th Gram block has eigenvalues
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{\pm,i}=\frac{\sigma_{A,i}^2}{2}
 \left(A_i\pm D_i\right),\quad
 A_i=1+\gamma_i^2,\quad
 D_i=\sqrt{(1-\gamma_i^2)^2+4\gamma_i^2\alpha_i}.
  \label{eq:discriminant}
\end{equation}
The corresponding singular values are $\sqrt{\lambda_{+,i}}$ and
$\sqrt{\lambda_{-,i}}$.  This result is exact under the stated pairing and
decoupling conditions (Appendix~\ref{app:proof_thm}).
\end{lemma}

The global ratio $\gamma=\|H_B\|_*/\|H_A\|_*$ does \emph{not} imply
$\gamma_i=\gamma$.  Replacing the collection $\{\gamma_i,\alpha_i\}$ by
global summaries $(\gamma,\alpha)$ is an approximation step.

\begin{proposition}[Exact Proportional-Spectrum Model]
\label{prop:proportional}
Under Lemma~\ref{lem:gram}, further assume
$\sigma_{B,i}=\gamma\sigma_{A,i}$ and $\alpha_i=\alpha$ for every $i$.
Let $A=1+\gamma^2$, $D=\sqrt{(1-\gamma^2)^2+4\gamma^2\alpha}$, and
\begin{equation}
 q = \frac{\sqrt{(A+D)/2}}
 {\sqrt{(A+D)/2}+\sqrt{(A-D)/2}}.
  \label{eq:rstar}
\end{equation}
Then the merged spectrum consists of two scaled copies of the spectrum of
$H_A$, and
\begin{equation}
 r_{\mathrm{merged}}=r_A\exp(H_b(q)),\qquad r_A=r_B.
 \label{eq:proportional_exact}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Collapse Summary Predictor]
\label{def:collapse}
For general spectra, we compress the mode-wise quantities to the global
nuclear-norm ratio $\gamma$ and mean top-$k$ alignment $\alpha$, compute
$q$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:rstar}, and define
\begin{equation}
  \hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}} =
  \exp\!\Big(H_b(q) + q\log r_{\mathrm{dom}} + (1-q)\log r_{\mathrm{sub}}\Big),
  \label{eq:pred}
\end{equation}
where $H_b(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)$.
\end{definition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:pred} is a theory-motivated low-dimensional
approximation, not an identity or a universally bounded estimator.  It
interpolates the input spectral entropies and adds the binary entropy of
the idealized two-branch split.  Its accuracy on general spectra is an
empirical question evaluated in Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\textbf{Interpretation.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:pred} is a weighted log-geometric mean of the
individual ranks plus a balance bonus $H_b(q)$ (maximal when
$q{=}0.5$, vanishing as $q{\to}0,1$).

\textbf{Behavior regimes.}
\emph{Superadditive} ($\alpha{\approx}0$, $\gamma{\approx}1$): $q{\approx}0.5$,
$\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}{\approx}2\sqrt{r_Ar_B}\geq r_{\mathrm{dom}}$.
\emph{Directional collapse} ($\alpha{\to}1$): $q{\to}1$, $H_b(q){\to}0$,
$\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}{\to}r_{\mathrm{dom}}$.
\emph{Energy collapse} ($\gamma{\gg}1$ or $\gamma{\ll}1$): $q{\to}1$,
$\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}{\to}r_{\mathrm{dom}}$.
When $\alpha{\to}1$ \emph{and} $\gamma{\gg}1$, the merged rank can fall
\emph{below} $r_{\mathrm{sub}}$---a net loss in diversity.

\subsection{Superadditivity Threshold}
\label{sec:superadd}

\begin{corollary}[Summary-Model Superadditivity Diagnostic]
\label{cor:superadd}
The summary predictor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred} predicts
$\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}>r_{\mathrm{dom}}$
for $q<1$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
  \frac{r_{\mathrm{dom}}}{r_{\mathrm{sub}}} < \rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)
  \;\equiv\; \exp\!\!\left(\frac{H_b(q)}{1-q}\right),
  \label{eq:rho_c}
\end{equation}
where $q$ is from Eq.~\eqref{eq:rstar}.
The threshold $\rho_c$ is increasing in $\alpha$:
$\rho_c\geq4.0$ for all $\alpha\in[0,0.9]$ at $\gamma=1$.
As $q\to1^{-}$, $\rho_c\to\infty$ while the predicted gain tends to zero.
At the degenerate point $q=1$ the division in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho_c} is
undefined and Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred} gives
$\hat r_{\mathrm{merged}}=r_{\mathrm{dom}}$ exactly.
\end{corollary}

This is an exact decision boundary for the summary model, and for the
proportional-spectrum model of Proposition~\ref{prop:proportional}; on
general data it is a diagnostic rather than a guarantee.

\textbf{Physical meaning.}
$\rho_c$ is a decision threshold internal to the summary model.  Near
$q=1$, it can classify an arbitrarily small positive entropy increment as
superadditive; the magnitude $\hat r_{\mathrm{merged}}-r_{\mathrm{dom}}$
must therefore be reported alongside the binary diagnosis.  Exact directional
overlap gives $q=1$ and no predicted gain.
The proofs and model scope are in
Appendices~\ref{app:proof_thm},~\ref{app:pa_scope}.

\subsection{Marginal Spectra and Principal Angles Are Not Sufficient}
\label{sec:insufficiency}

\begin{proposition}[Non-identifiability from Marginal Geometry]
\label{prop:insufficient}
Even the two marginal singular spectra together with the complete set of
principal angles between the right singular subspaces do not uniquely
determine $\reff([H_A;H_B])$.  Consequently, neither does the tuple
$(r_A,r_B,\gamma,\alpha)$.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Counterexample.}
Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be standard basis vectors and define the row-normalized
matrices
\begin{equation}
 H_A=[e_1,e_1,e_1,e_1,e_2]^\top,\quad
 H_B^{(1)}=[e_1,e_1,e_1,e_1,e_3]^\top,\quad
 H_B^{(2)}=[e_1,e_3,e_3,e_3,e_3]^\top.
\end{equation}
All three have nonzero singular values $(2,1)$.  Both right-subspace pairs
have principal angles $\{0,\pi/2\}$, yet their merged Gram spectra are
$(8,1,1)$ and $(5,4,1)$, respectively.  The corresponding effective ranks
are $2.626012$ and $2.849536$.  Thus even complete marginal spectra and
principal angles omit how spectral mass is assigned to coupled directions;
the four scalar summaries discard still more information.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Composable Rank-$L$ Gram Sketch}
\label{sec:rank_l}

The counterexample motivates approximating the PSD operator itself rather
than compressing it to averaged angles.  For the compact SVD of each
preweighted source, define
\begin{equation}
 B_j=\Sigma_{j,1:L}V_{j,1:L}^{\top},\qquad
 \widetilde G_j=B_j^\top B_j,\qquad
 E_j=G_j-\widetilde G_j\succeq0 .
 \label{eq:rank_l_sketch}
\end{equation}
Attach the tail statistics
$\tau_j=\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{E_j}$,
$\omega_j=\operatorname{tr}(E_j)$, and
$u_j=\operatorname{rank}(E_j)$; certified upper bounds may replace their
exact values.  For a subset $S$, vertically stack the factors into $B_S$.
Then $\widetilde G_S=B_S^\top B_S$, so its nonzero spectrum is obtained
from the small core $B_SB_S^\top$ without reconstructing sample features.

For a PSD matrix $G$, write
$\mathcal R(G)=\exp\mathcal H(\sqrt{\lambda(G)}/
\operatorname{tr}\sqrt G)$; hence
$\mathcal R(H^\top H)=\reff(H)$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 T_S=\min\!\left\{\sum_{j\in S}\tau_j,
 \sqrt{\min(d,\sum_{j\in S}u_j)\sum_{j\in S}\omega_j}\right\},
 \quad
 \varepsilon_S=\frac{T_S}{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{\widetilde G_S}+T_S}.
 \label{eq:tail_bound}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Rank-$L$ Log-Effective-Rank Bound]
\label{thm:rank_l_bound}
Assume $\widetilde G_S\neq0$, and let
$D_S=\min(d,\sum_{j\in S}\operatorname{rank}(G_j))$.  For $D\ge2$, set
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_D(\varepsilon)=
 \begin{cases}
 h_b(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\log(D-1),
 &0\leq\varepsilon\leq1-1/D,\\
 \log D,&1-1/D<\varepsilon\leq1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Then, without a block-compatibility assumption,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\log\mathcal R(G_S)-\log\mathcal R(\widetilde G_S)\right|
 \leq \Phi_{D_S}(\varepsilon_S).
 \label{eq:rank_l_bound}
\end{equation}
For $D_S=1$ the error is zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[One-Step Full-Gram Certificate]
\label{cor:rank_l_choice}
At a greedy step, let
$\widehat F_j=\log\mathcal R(\widetilde G_{S\cup\{j\}})$ and
$e_j=\Phi_{D_{S\cup\{j\}}}(\varepsilon_{S\cup\{j\}})$.
If a candidate $j^*$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F_{j^*}-e_{j^*}>
 \max_{j\neq j^*}(\widehat F_j+e_j),
 \label{eq:rank_l_certificate}
\end{equation}
then $j^*$ uniquely maximizes the exact Full-Gram score in that round.
\end{corollary}

The interval accumulates tails from the selected set and the candidate;
using only the candidate tail is invalid.  Failure to separate intervals is
a diagnostic to increase $L$ or fall back to Full-Gram scoring.  The
certificate is local to one greedy step: effective rank is generally
nonmonotone and nonsubmodular, so it gives no global subset-optimality claim
(Appendix~\ref{app:optimization_limits}).

\textbf{Legacy Collapse-4S calibration.}
On the evaluated real-data pairs Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred} systematically
over-predicts.  A fitted multiplicative constant reduces average scale error;
the reported relation $c{=}0.946{-}0.175\log(N/\reff)$ is an empirical fit
over the evaluated encoder families, not a universal law
(Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}).

\section{Evaluation of the Legacy Four-Summary Predictor}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section reports the historical evaluation of Collapse-4S.  These
experiments test Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred}, not the rank-$L$ approximation or its
certificate.  We organize the evidence as:
(i)~\textbf{idealized-model agreement}---the predictor agrees with data
generated under its summary assumptions (§\ref{sec:e1a_main}, synthetic grid);
(ii)~\textbf{real-data association}---the formula remains correlated with
approximation across 5 encoder families, 14 vision datasets,
and 19 text corpora (§\ref{sec:e1d}, 427 pairs);
(iii)~\textbf{behavioral regime agreement}---empirical superadditivity
patterns match the three regimes predicted by
Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd} (§\ref{sec:e1d}, Table~\ref{tab:e1d}).
Together these locate useful and failing regimes of the heuristic.
Additional controls (E1a-Ind 120 non-principal-angle pairs,
E1b 30 real pairs, E1c 57 pairs, E\_diverse cross-architecture,
E\_downstream $\reff$/LP-accuracy) all agree with the same picture
and are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:exp_extras}.
Feature matrices are L2-row-normalized and subsampled to
$N_{\mathrm{sub}}$ samples; effective rank uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:reff};
bootstrap CIs use $B=10{,}000$ resamples.

\section{Two-Stage Data-Pool Compression}
\label{sec:applications}
\label{sec:compression}
\label{sec:pool_selection}

The objective of Stage~1 is a label-blind shortlist, not a final utility
decision.  Collapse-Gram-$L$ applies Algorithm~\ref{alg:collapse_greedy} to
the composable sketches of Sec.~\ref{sec:rank_l}.  In the current Stage~2
audit, each candidate adapter is trained with that source's labels; repeated
cross-validation inside target-train fits ridge probes and selects the source.
The target-test split is opened once after selection is frozen.  This is a
source-supervised adaptation protocol with target-validation selection, not a
target-trained adapter.  The separation is necessary because spectral
diversity alone cannot determine arbitrary target utility.

\paragraph{Corrected protocol.}
Source rows for Stage~1 are sampled without labels, and unequal-size sources
use the declared equal-source weighting $X_j=H_j/\sqrt{N_j}$
($\beta_j=1$).  We compare
Collapse-Gram-$L$ against rank-only, DPP, Facility Location, agglomerative
medoids, leverage-score selection, random selection, and Full-Gram greedy;
an exhaustive merged-rank oracle is included only when the predeclared
combination-count limit permits it.  All Stage-2 candidates receive
identical source-supervised optimization steps and seeds, with frozen-identity
and untrained random-adapter controls.  Target-train cross-validation is the
only target signal used before selection and all adapter checkpoints are
hashed.  A selection manifest is then written and hashed before a separate
held-out command is permitted to open the target-test cache.  Manifests bind
code, inputs, preprocessing, weights,
sampled indices, checkpoints, and hyperparameters by SHA-256; incomplete
source/seed groups are never resumed as completed runs.  Overlap detection is
reported against same-dataset matched negatives, and the $0\%$ overlap
condition has no parent ground truth.  To estimate exhaustive shortlist
regret, the audit trained all candidate adapters offline; its total wall time
is therefore not a measured deployment saving.

\paragraph{Audited historical pilot.}
The pre-correction 21-source pilot used label-stratified source caches,
queried target test before freezing selection, and did not evaluate
Collapse-Gram-$L$.  Its DPP and rank-only Recall@10 were both $85.71\%$, with
strong near-tie sensitivity; we retain it only as a historical audit baseline
and report the corrected results below.

\paragraph{Corrected controlled evidence.}
An intermediate \texttt{corrected\_v2} run was invalidated because of a
statistic-definition mismatch and incomplete provenance.  In the replacement
\texttt{corrected\_v3} sweep, four encoders, ten construction seeds, six
overlap levels, and four budgets give 960 configurations per rank.  Table~\ref{tab:v3_rank_sweep}
reports macro-averages over encoders.  Every selected-prefix interval contains
the exact score, but all intervals overlap at every evaluated decision.  The
bound is valid yet operationally vacuous at these ranks.  DPP is stronger in
this controlled spectral-retention task; this is a recorded unequal-byte
comparison, not a communication-matched Pareto result.
A separate $L=20$ exact-reference pilot (four encoders, three seeds, 288
configurations, Random-100) gives rank-$L$/DPP/Full-Gram gains of
$3.65/5.37/5.81\%$ over rank-only.  Its intervals cover $288/288$ prefixes,
but certify $0/720$ steps and match $0/288$ Full-Gram ordered prefixes;
Full-Gram here is a greedy reference, not a global oracle.

\paragraph{Corrected held-out utility evidence.}
The natural-pool rerun contains 21 sources, 7 targets, 4 encoders, and 5
adapter seeds.  At shortlist $10/21$, strict oracle recall is $75.00\%$ for
rank-$L$, $85.71\%$ for rank-only and DPP, and $89.29\%$ for Full-Gram greedy;
none reaches the predeclared $90\%$ threshold.  Their held-out regrets within
the adapter family are small ($0.00022$--$0.00082$), but the family itself
fails the source-independent control.  Mean held-out accuracy is $0.83654$ for
frozen identity, $0.77144$ for an untrained random adapter, and $0.76951$ for
the validation-oracle source-supervised adapter.  Oracle minus identity is

selected adapter loses to identity in all 28 encoder-target units.  Thus small
regret cannot be interpreted as preserved utility or beneficial data use.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The theory controls spectral-score approximation only.  The corrected sweep
confirms interval coverage but shows that coverage alone need not produce a
usable decision certificate.  Stage~2 then rejects the current
source-supervised adapter family before any practical-compression claim is
made.  Exact identity, tie-aware recall, regret, and source-independent utility
controls are therefore separate quantities.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We separated exact spectral structure, bounded approximation, and
task-dependent evidence.  Gram additivity and the block-compatible local model
are exact; the counterexample rules out universal prediction from the legacy
summaries.  The rank-$L$ sketch has a valid deterministic error interval, but
the corrected sweep obtains zero decision certificates for $L\leq50$ and finds
DPP at least as competitive.  The held-out audit is more decisive: even the
validation-oracle source-supervised adapter is substantially worse than frozen
identity.  The current contribution is therefore an exact local analysis, an
auditable approximation protocol, and a documented negative result---not a
validated practical compressor.  Equal-byte Pareto evidence and a downstream
adapter family that first beats identity are prerequisites for a positive
application claim.

\section{Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}

The four summaries $(r_A,r_B,\gamma,\alpha)$ are not sufficient to
determine merged effective rank in general
(Proposition~\ref{prop:insufficient}).  The exact $2\times2$ decomposition
requires mode-wise pairing and decoupling; the closed entropy expression
additionally requires proportional spectra and uniform alignment.  We do
not claim a universal error bound for Collapse-4S.  The empirical
calibration relation (Eq.~\ref{eq:universal_c}) is fitted on few encoder
families and transfers poorly to SBERT.  The rank-$L$ theorem requires an
exact PSD truncation (or valid tail upper bounds) under fixed,
subset-independent preprocessing; data-dependent global centering would
break direct composability.  Its intervals are too wide to certify any tested
choice at $L\leq50$, and increasing $L$ increases communication; the current
baselines are not equal-byte matched.  Forming a Gram matrix also squares the
condition number, so extremely ill-conditioned inputs require direct-SVD
cross-checks or explicit numerical slack.  Finally, unlabeled geometry cannot
determine arbitrary labels.  The corrected natural pool remains domain
imbalanced, and the source-supervised bottleneck adapter is a narrow proxy that
fails against frozen identity.  LoRA and multi-pool training were consequently
not run under the preregistered stopping rule.  Broader domains and a useful
adapter family would be required before claiming practical utility.

\section{Proofs of the Exact Gram Results}
\label{app:proof_thm}

\textbf{Proposition~\ref{prop:gram_general}.}
From the compact SVDs,
$H_A^\top H_A=V_A\Sigma_A^2V_A^\top$ and
$H_B^\top H_B=V_B\Sigma_B^2V_B^\top$.
Because the first columns of $Q_A$ are $V_A$,
$Q_A^\top V_A\Sigma_A^2V_A^\top Q_A=\widetilde\Sigma_A^2$.
Writing $C=Q_A^\top V_B$ gives
$Q_A^\top V_B\Sigma_B^2V_B^\top Q_A=C\Sigma_B^2C^\top$.
Adding proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:general_gram}.  Orthogonal similarity preserves
eigenvalues, whose square roots are the merged singular values. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:gram}.}

Under paired, decoupled directions, the $i$-th right singular vector of $H_B$ is
$V_{B,i}=\cos\theta_i V_{A,i}+\sin\theta_i V_{\perp,i}$,
where $V_{A,i}$ is the $i$-th right singular vector of $H_A$
and the two-dimensional planes are mutually orthogonal.
In the basis $\{V_{A,i},V_{\perp,i}\}$:
\begin{align}
  H_A^\top H_A \text{ (block)} &= \sigma_{A,i}^2\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}, \\
  H_B^\top H_B \text{ (block)} &= \sigma_{B,i}^2
    \begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\sin\theta_i \\
    \cos\theta_i\sin\theta_i & \sin^2\theta_i\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
Define the mode-wise ratio $\gamma_i=\sigma_{B,i}/\sigma_{A,i}$; no
global proportional-spectrum assumption is required.  Summing gives
\begin{equation}
  G_i = \sigma_{A,i}^2\begin{pmatrix}1+\gamma_i^2\alpha_i & \gamma_i\sqrt{\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)} \\
    \gamma_i\sqrt{\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)} & \gamma_i^2(1-\alpha_i)\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_i=\cos^2\theta_i$.
Its trace is $\sigma_{A,i}^2(1+\gamma_i^2)$ and determinant is
$\sigma_{A,i}^4\gamma_i^2(1-\alpha_i)$, yielding
Eq.~\eqref{eq:discriminant} by the quadratic formula. $\square$

\textbf{Proposition~\ref{prop:proportional}.}

With $\gamma_i=\gamma$ and $\alpha_i=\alpha$, define
$c_\pm=\sqrt{(A\pm D)/2}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} gives merged
singular values $\{c_+\sigma_{A,i}\}_i\cup\{c_-\sigma_{A,i}\}_i$.
After nuclear-norm normalization their probabilities are
$q p_i^A$ and $(1-q)p_i^A$, where
$q=c_+/(c_++c_-)$.  Hence
\begin{align}
 H_{\mathrm{merged}}
 &=H_b(q)+qH(p^A)+(1-q)H(p^A)\\
 &=H_b(q)+\log r_A.
\end{align}
Exponentiating proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:proportional_exact}.  Proportional
spectra have identical normalized singular-value distributions, so
$r_A=r_B$. $\square$

\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:rank_l_bound}.}
Write $E_S=\sum_{j\in S}E_j$.  For PSD matrices,
$\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{A+B}\leq\operatorname{tr}\sqrt A+
\operatorname{tr}\sqrt B$: apply the nuclear-norm triangle inequality to
$[A^{1/2},B^{1/2}]$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{tr}\sqrt{E_S}\leq\sum_j\tau_j,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}\sqrt{E_S}
 \leq\sqrt{\operatorname{rank}(E_S)\operatorname{tr}(E_S)}
 \leq\sqrt{\min(d,\sum_j u_j)\sum_j\omega_j},
\end{equation}
so $\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{E_S}\leq T_S$.  Let
$a_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i(\widetilde G_S)}$ and
$b_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i(G_S)}$ in decreasing order.  Since
$G_S\succeq\widetilde G_S$, eigenvalue monotonicity gives $b_i\geq a_i$.
With $\widetilde\nu=\sum_i a_i$ and
$\delta=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)$, trace subadditivity gives $0\leq\delta\leq T_S$.
If $\delta>0$, the normalized true spectrum is
\begin{equation}
 p=\frac{\widetilde\nu}{\widetilde\nu+\delta}\widetilde p
 +\frac{\delta}{\widetilde\nu+\delta}q,
 \qquad q_i=\frac{b_i-a_i}{\delta}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\operatorname{TV}(p,\widetilde p)\leq
\delta/(\widetilde\nu+\delta)\leq\varepsilon_S$.
Fannes--Audenaert entropy continuity on a support of size at most $D_S$
gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:rank_l_bound}.  The $\delta=0$ and $D_S=1$ cases are
immediate. $\square$

\textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:rank_l_choice}.}
The theorem places every exact candidate score in
$[\widehat F_j-e_j,\widehat F_j+e_j]$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:rank_l_certificate}
puts the lower endpoint of $j^*$ strictly above every competing upper
endpoint. $\square$

\textbf{Status of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred}.}
For unequal or non-proportional spectra, the two branches need not inherit
the input distributions, and $q$ can vary by mode.  Equation~\eqref{eq:pred}
therefore defines a structured approximation; no exactness or universal
error bound is asserted for it.

\section{Optimization Limits}
\label{app:optimization_limits}

Effective-rank maximization is not generally monotone.  Take
$G_A=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/2)$ and
$G_B=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$.  Then $\mathcal R(G_A)=2$, whereas
$\mathcal R(G_A+G_B)=1.928623<2$.  It is also not generally submodular.
Let $G_0=\operatorname{diag}(1/5,4/5)$,
$G_1=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$, and
$G_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$.  For
$A=\{0\}\subset B=\{0,1\}$, direct evaluation of
$F(S)=\log\mathcal R(\sum_{j\in S}G_j)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 F(A\cup\{2\})-F(A)=0.051522
 <0.125701=F(B\cup\{2\})-F(B),
\end{equation}
violating diminishing returns.
Thus standard monotone-submodular greedy guarantees do not apply; exhaustive
search is used only as a finite small-pool reference.

\section{Bimodal Principal-Angle Structure in Self-Supervised Encoders}
\label{app:bimodal}

The per-dimension $\cos^2\theta_i$ profiles and their prediction
consequences are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:bimodal_structure} (main text):
CLIP/DINO exhibit a two-cluster step structure (top-5 ratios
$20{\times}$/$31{\times}$ over bot-5, $V_\alpha{=}0.090$/$0.073$),
while ResNet-50 decays monotonically ($V_\alpha{=}0.019$).

\textbf{Two distinct mechanisms drive the low-$R^2$ probes.}
For DINO ($R^2{=}0.55$), the cause is the bimodal $\cos^2\theta_i$
analysed below. For ViT ($R^2{=}0.49$, supervised), the cause is
\emph{energy imbalance} rather than bimodality: $17/17$ ViT
non-superadditive pairs lie in $\gamma{\notin}[0.5,2]$ (cross-resolution
pairs such as MNIST $\times$ ImageNet), where the global energy summary is
extreme and the two-component mixture approximation degrades.
ViT's principal-angle distribution remains near-uniform (no bimodality);
its lower $R^2$ instead empirically marks the energy-imbalance regime as
a reliability boundary.
We retain the full ViT row in Table~\ref{tab:e1d} for transparency,
flagging $\gamma{\in}[0.5,2]$ as ViT's reliable applicability window.

\textbf{Honest scope.}
We treat the bimodal failure mode as a genuine boundary of the current
formula, not a feature.
The uniform-$\alpha$ assumption underlying
The global-summary approximation is stressed whenever the principal-angle
distribution is bimodal, and self-supervised contrastive/distillation
encoders (CLIP, DINO) systematically produce such distributions on
cross-domain pairs.
Within this regime, the calibrated formula \emph{still ranks correctly}
(Pearson $r{\geq}0.90$ on all four probes, Table~\ref{tab:e1d})
but the absolute prediction $\hat r$ is biased upward and the
superadditivity-decision boundary is shifted, explaining the
$29$--$36\%$ observed superadditivity rates on CLIP/DINO cross-domain
pairs.
\textbf{What the Collapse Model does contribute here} is mechanistic
localisation: the bimodal top/bottom ratio ${>}20{\times}$ in
$\cos^2\theta_i$ (Figure~\ref{fig:bimodal_structure}) exposes alignment
heterogeneity discarded by the scalar summary, rather than leaving the
failure as a black-box surprise.

\textbf{What does not work.}
A naive energy-weighted patch
$\bar\alpha_w{=}\sum_i(\sigma_i/\|H\|_*)\cos^2\theta_i$
worsens predictions (Pearson $r$ drops $1$--$2\%$, MAE rises
$6$--$8\%$), because it overweights the most-aligned direction's
redundancy while suppressing intermediate-direction contributions.

\textbf{What we believe will work, and how to test it.}
The natural next step is a \emph{piecewise paired-direction model}:
partition the $k$ directions into a high-alignment cluster
($\cos^2\theta_i{\geq}\tau$) and a low-alignment cluster, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} separately within each cluster, and combine the
resulting branch spectra.  This retains more of the distribution of
$\alpha_i$ than a single mean, but it still requires controlling spectral
pairing and cross-direction coupling.  We leave this extension and its
error analysis to future work.

\section{$r_A{+}r_B$ Heuristic: Safe Regime and Failure Mode}
\label{app:rsum_regime}

\paragraph{When does $r_A{+}r_B$ suffice, and when does it fail?}
On clean curated pools, the $r_A{+}r_B$ and Max-$\reff$ heuristics
often select the same subset as Collapse-4S (within $0.7$\,pp in the
historical $M{=}43$ stress test).
This agreement is consistent with the summary diagnostic in
Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}: when all top-$\reff$ candidates have low
mutual alignment ($\bar\alpha{<}0.2$, i.e.\ rank-ratio $r_{\mathrm{dom}}/r_{\mathrm{sub}}{<}\rho_c$),
the superadditivity bonus $H_b(q)$ is dominated by the rank term
$q\log r_{\mathrm{dom}}{+}(1{-}q)\log r_{\mathrm{sub}}$,
making individual rank a near-sufficient statistic.
Collapse-4S thus serves a different role than ``beating $r_A{+}r_B$
everywhere'':
\textbf{(i)~A priori low-redundancy diagnostic.}
$\rho_c(\bar\alpha,\bar\gamma)$ computed from per-dataset summaries
\emph{indicates} when $r_A{+}r_B$ is likely to agree without actually
running competing strategies.
On our ResNet-50 vision pools, $\bar\alpha{<}0.2$ between top-$\reff$
pairs places $r_{\mathrm{dom}}/r_{\mathrm{sub}}$ well inside the predicted
agreement regime ($\rho_c{>}4$ at $\bar\alpha{\leq}0.2$,
Cor.~\ref{cor:superadd}), matching the observed heuristic-equivalence
at $M{=}14$ and $M{=}43$.
\textbf{(ii)~Detection of the unsafe regime.}
When candidates contain near-duplicate or strongly aligned subspaces, the
$\alpha$ channel supplies information that rank-only omits.
The clone experiment below ($r_A{+}r_B$ retention drops $25.5\%$,
Collapse holds) demonstrates this on a controlled stress test;
the naturally bimodal CLIP/DINO cross-domain pairs
(Sec.~\ref{sec:e1d}, $\alpha\in[0.35,0.41]$ with top-5 $\cos^2\theta\geq0.7$)
are real-world settings where the $\alpha$ signal is non-negligible
and the rank-only heuristic can fail.
\textbf{(iii)~Diagnosis without execution.}
The Collapse-4S regime classification is computable from $(\gamma,\alpha)$,
but Proposition~\ref{prop:insufficient} makes it a heuristic diagnostic, not
a certified decision rule.

\paragraph{Redundancy clone trap: failure mode of $r_A{+}r_B$.}
To stress-test the alignment term under constructed redundancy,
we construct a \emph{clone attack} scenario: the highest-$\reff$ dataset
in the pool (STL-10, $\reff{=}994$) is cloned via random rotation
$H_{\mathrm{clone}}{=}H_{\mathrm{orig}}Q(\theta)$
($\theta\in\{0.01,0.03,0.05,0.07,0.10\}$ radians), producing 5 clones
that preserve the original $\reff$ but are highly aligned with the
original ($\SAk{>}0.99$).
On a pool of 5 real + 5 cloned ResNet-50 datasets, $r_A{+}r_B$ greedy
at $k{=}3$ selects $2/3$ clones ($\reff^{\mathrm{merged}}{=}997$),
while Collapse-4S assigns a redundancy penalty via high $\alpha$,
\textbf{selecting $0/3$ clones}
($\reff^{\mathrm{merged}}{=}1251$, $+25.5\%$).
At $k{=}5$ the gap persists: $r_A{+}r_B$ selects $4/5$ clones
($\reff{=}998$), Collapse only $2/5$ ($\reff{=}1233$).
This result is consistent across all 5 $\theta$ values
($\theta{=}0.01$--$0.10$, $\SAk$ from $0.9999$ to $0.9891$).
Because the duplicated source was deliberately chosen from the highest-rank
candidate, this is an adversarial stress test rather than evidence about
natural-pool frequency.  It shows only that alignment can help in this
constructed case.

\section{Scope of the Paired-Direction Model}
\label{app:pa_scope}
\label{app:generalization}
\label{app:calibration}
\label{app:algorithm}

\begin{remark}[Exact and approximate layers]
Proposition~\ref{prop:gram_general} is unconditional and retains the full
coupling matrix $C=Q_A^\top V_B$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} is exact only when
the singular directions can be paired into mutually orthogonal
two-dimensional planes.  Proposition~\ref{prop:proportional} additionally
requires mode-wise proportional spectra and uniform alignment; these
conditions imply $r_A=r_B$.  The practical predictor
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred} allows $r_A\neq r_B$ by interpolating two input
entropies, and is therefore a modeling approximation rather than a
consequence of the proportional-spectrum proposition.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Information discarded by global summaries]
The global ratio $\gamma=\|H_B\|_*/\|H_A\|_*$ does not imply
$\sigma_{B,i}=\gamma\sigma_{A,i}$.  Likewise,
$\alpha=\SAk$ does not retain the distribution of $\alpha_i$, its
association with spectral mass, or dense cross-direction coupling.
Proposition~\ref{prop:insufficient} gives an explicit pair of examples
with identical $(r_A,r_B,\gamma,\alpha)$ and different merged effective
ranks.  Consequently no estimator based only on these four scalars can be
universally exact.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Empirical generalization]
The summaries in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pred} remain well-defined outside the
paired-direction model.  Their empirical correlations on synthetic
out-of-model generators and real encoders quantify predictive utility,
not theorem validity.  Alignment heterogeneity, non-proportional spectra,
and cross-direction coupling are observable failure sources; calibration
corrects average scale on the evaluated encoder family but does not create
a general guarantee.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Calibrated Collapse Model]
\label{rem:calibration}
On real data the paired-direction summary model is approximate---cross-pair
coupling, non-proportional spectra ($\sigma_{B,i}\neq\gamma\sigma_{A,i}$,
hence $r_A\neq r_B$), and higher-order interactions introduce
a systematic upward bias on the evaluated pairs.
Empirically the over-prediction ratio is $1.24$--$1.84\times$ (E1c),
and its gap is positively associated with alignment heterogeneity.
Since the log-space bias has low variance
($\mathrm{std}=0.164$ vs.\ $\mathrm{mean}=0.293$),
a single multiplicative calibration restores quantitative accuracy:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{r}_{\mathrm{cal}} = c\cdot\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}},\quad
  c = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\log\frac{\hat{r}_i}{r_i}\right).
  \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
This calibration compensates for the entropy over-estimation
caused by structure that the four global summaries ignore.
With $c{=}0.746$ estimated from E1c ($n{=}57$),
LOO-CV yields $R^2{=}0.855$, RMSE${=}87.7$ (vs.\ uncalibrated $R^2{=}0.049$,
RMSE${=}224$; Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration}).
The constant generalizes across datasets within each encoder family:
calibrating on the 30 E1b pairs ($c{=}0.768$)
and testing on the held-out 57 E1c pairs yields $R^2{=}0.859$, RMSE${=}86.3$
(Table~\ref{tab:calibration}).
However, $c$ varies across encoder families
($c{=}0.78$ for ResNet-50 vs.\ $c{=}0.65$ for ViT-B/16),
reflecting systematic differences in how each architecture's spectral structure
deviates from the paired-direction summary model.
On the evaluated encoder families, this variation is fit by:
\begin{equation}
  c \;=\; 0.946 - 0.175\,\log(N/\reff),
  \quad R^2{=}0.963,\;\text{LOO mean error}{=}0.027,
  \label{eq:universal_c}
\end{equation}
This small-sample regression is descriptive and requires validation on a
new encoder family before use; its in-sample $R^2$ is not evidence of a
universal law.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why a Task-Aware Second Stage Is Necessary}
\label{sec:transfer_connection}

Unlabeled Grams contain no information about an arbitrary target labeling.
Indeed, two tasks may share exactly the same frozen features while assigning
labels to different directions, reversing the utility order of two source
subsets.  Therefore no label-free spectral score can universally guarantee
downstream utility.  Our Stage-2 validation is not a consequence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rank_l_bound}: it is the separate empirical step needed to
measure target-conditioned utility under equal training compute.


\paragraph{Certified Stage-1 pseudocode.}

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Collapse-Gram-$L$ Greedy Screening}
\label{alg:collapse_greedy}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Summaries $\{(B_j,\tau_j,\omega_j,u_j)\}_{j=1}^M$, budget $K$
\Ensure Ordered shortlist $S$, with a certificate flag for each step
\State $S\gets\emptyset$;\quad $C\gets\{1,\ldots,M\}$
\For{$t = 1,\ldots,K$}
  \For{each $j \in C$}
    \State compute $\widehat F_j$ and $e_j$ for $S\cup\{j\}$ using
      Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rank_l_sketch}--\eqref{eq:rank_l_bound}
  \EndFor
  \State $j^*\gets\arg\max_{j\in C}\widehat F_j$
  \State $z_t\gets[\widehat F_{j^*}-e_{j^*}>
    \max_{j\neq j^*}(\widehat F_j+e_j)]$
  \State $S\gets S\mathbin{\|}j^*$;\quad $C\gets C\setminus\{j^*\}$
\EndFor
\State \Return $(S,z_1,\ldots,z_K)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{remark}[Formula as directional oracle, not absolute predictor]
\label{rem:r2_vs_pearson}
The contrast between $R^2=-1.09$ (overall, E1a-Ind) and Pearson $r=0.9987$ (same data)
is not contradictory: $R^2$ measures absolute calibration, while Pearson $r$ measures
directional ranking.
This distinction is crucial for the real-data results (E1c): the formula yields
$R^2\approx0.05$ (poor absolute calibration, $1.24$--$1.84\times$ over-prediction)
but Pearson $r=0.928$ (correct directional ranking).
For practical applications---ranking candidate merges by diversity gain,
dataset selection, data valuation---\emph{ranking} is the operationally relevant metric,
not absolute $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ prediction.
Absolute calibration requires a correction factor (calibrated $\hat{r}=0.81\cdot\hat{r}_{\mathrm{pred}}$,
$R^2_{\mathrm{cal}}=0.862$ on E1c data), which we report as a practical recommendation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{E1b: Real Dataset Pairs (30 Pairs)}
\label{sec:e1b}

\textbf{Design.}
We evaluate all $\binom{6}{2}=15$ pairs from
$\{$CIFAR-10, STL-10, SVHN, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-100$\}$
using 2 pretrained probes (ResNet-50 \citep{he2016resnet} $d{=}2048$
and ViT-B/16 \citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} $d{=}768$),
$N_{\mathrm{sub}}=500$ samples per dataset: \textbf{30 total configurations}.
The Collapse Model is applied with per-probe nuclear-norm $\gamma$.

\textbf{Results.}
\textbf{86.7\%} of pairs are superadditive (26/30; 95\% CI [73\%, 97\%]).
Pearson $r=0.948$ (95\% CI [0.906, 0.978]) between predicted and actual merged rank.
Per-probe: ResNet-50 achieves 100\% superadditivity (15/15, Pearson $r=0.968$);
ViT-B/16 achieves 73.3\% (11/15, Pearson $r=0.945$).

\textbf{All 4 exceptions are ViT-B/16 pairs involving CIFAR-100.}
The exceptions are associated with \emph{energy imbalance}, consistent with
the superadditivity diagnostic (Corollary~\ref{cor:superadd}).
CIFAR-100 has 100 classes and correspondingly high intrinsic complexity:
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}(\text{CIFAR-100})\approx526$ under ViT-B/16, far exceeding
partner datasets such as MNIST ($r_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx140$) and SVHN ($\approx243$).
This yields energy ratios $\gamma\in[2.0,\,2.5]$, placing these pairs
near the superadditivity boundary $\rho_c(\alpha,\gamma)$.
Specifically, the dominant component absorbs the subordinate:
$q\to1$, $H_b(q)\to0$, and $\hat{r}_{\mathrm{merged}}\to r_{\mathrm{dom}}$,
so the observed $r_{\mathrm{merged}}$ can fall slightly below $r_{\mathrm{dom}}$
due to spectral averaging.
We verify this by sweeping $N_{\mathrm{sub}}\in\{500,1000,2000,4000\}$:
at $N{=}500$, CIFAR-100's $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is compressed to $\approx343$
(comparable to partners), giving $\gamma\approx1$ and 100\% superadditivity (5/5);
as $N$ grows to 2000+, $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ stabilizes at 526, $\gamma$ increases,
and exceptions emerge.  These quantities co-vary and are consistent with the
energy-imbalance explanation, but the sweep does not identify it causally.
ResNet-50 ($d{=}2048$, $r_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx988$ for CIFAR-100)
yields even larger $\gamma$ ratios, yet remains 100\% superadditive (36/36)
because its partner datasets also have higher $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ in 2048-dimensional space,
keeping $r_{\mathrm{dom}}/r_{\mathrm{sub}}$ below $\rho_c$.

\textbf{Key findings.}
(i)~At 427 evaluated pairs the overall Pearson correlation is $r{=}0.970$,
compared with $r{=}0.928$ in E1c.  Because pairs share datasets and the larger
set is not an independent replication, this increase does not validate a theorem.
(ii)~All 20 ImageNet-only pairs
are superadditive, and all 30 natural$\times$ImageNet cross-domain pairs
are superadditive; this is an empirical property of these extracted features.
(iii)~\textbf{ResNet-50 achieves 100\% superadditivity} across 91 pairs
(14 datasets including medical and ImageNet) on this benchmark.
In contrast, CLIP ViT-B/32 and DINO ViT-S/16 achieve only 31\% and 38\%
superadditivity respectively.
Analysis reveals a clear pattern: \emph{within-domain} pairs
(natural$\times$natural, ImageNet$\times$ImageNet) achieve near-perfect
superadditivity (CLIP: 90\% on ImageNet-only, 100\% on natural-only;
DINO: 100\% on both), while \emph{cross-domain} pairs
(handwritten$\times$ImageNet, natural$\times$digit) fail for nearly all
evaluated pairs.
This suggests that self-supervised encoders learn domain-specific spectral
structures with heterogeneous alignment and spectral coupling discarded by
the global summaries---an important boundary condition
for the Collapse Model's applicability.
(iv)~On 171 SBERT text pairs the correlation drops to $r{=}0.793$
(98.8\% superadditive).  The narrow $\reff$ range (IQR: $285$--$298$) may
contribute, but these results do not establish cross-modal generalization.
(v)~\textbf{Calibration constant varies by encoder family} ($c{\in}[0.57, 0.78]$),
but a single global $c{=}0.712$ achieves $R^2{=}0.898$ across all 158 vision pairs,
describing the average bias on this benchmark.

\textbf{Understanding non-superadditive pairs.}
ViT-B/16 achieves only 69\% superadditivity (38/55), lower than
ResNet-50's 100\% (91/91).
Analysis of the 17 ViT failures reveals a clear pattern:
\textbf{all failures occur in the energy-imbalance regime} ($\gamma\gg1$ or $\gamma\ll1$).
At $\gamma\geq3$, 80\% of pairs fail (8/10);
at $\gamma\in[0.5,1.5)$, only 4\% fail (1/24).
The failures concentrate in \emph{handwritten$\times$ImageNet} cross-domain pairs
(10/10 fail, 100\%)---these combine low-rank handwritten features
($\reff{\approx}140$ for MNIST via ViT-B/16) with high-rank ImageNet features
($\reff{\approx}600$), creating $\gamma\in[2.0,4.6]$ far beyond the
balanced regime where the two-component spectral mixture is accurate.
At large scale (55 pairs each), CLIP ViT-B/32 and DINO ViT-S/16
show only 29\% and 36\% superadditivity overall, with failures
concentrating in \emph{cross-domain} pairs:
handwritten$\times$ImageNet (100\% fail),
natural$\times$digit (100\% fail),
natural$\times$handwritten (100\% fail).
Within-domain pairs (natural-only, ImageNet-only) remain near-perfect.

\textbf{Root cause: bimodal principal angles.}
Comparing the \emph{same} dataset pair across encoders reveals
that the failure is not caused by energy imbalance ($\gamma$)
but by a structural violation of the uniform-$\alpha$ assumption.
Three mechanisms interact:

\emph{(a) Systematically higher alignment.}
On the same pair (e.g., CIFAR-10$\times$ImageNet-animal),
CLIP's subspace alignment $\alpha{=}0.41$ is $2.7\times$
higher than ResNet-50's $\alpha{=}0.15$.
Contrastive pre-training (CLIP) and self-distillation (DINO)
push cross-domain features into a shared semantic subspace,
creating high directional overlap where ResNet-50 preserves
domain-specific directions.

\emph{(b) Extreme spectral concentration.}
CLIP/DINO feature spectra are highly non-uniform:
$\kappa{=}15{,}000$--$1{,}200{,}000$ vs.\ ResNet-50's $\kappa{=}70$--$400$.
This concentration makes average alignment less informative because overlap
on a few high-mass directions can dominate many complementary tail directions.

\emph{(c) Bimodal principal-angle distribution.}
The \emph{individual} principal angles $\cos^2\theta_i$
are not approximately uniform (as the model assumes) but
\emph{bimodal}: the top-5 directions are nearly aligned
($\overline{\cos^2\theta}\approx0.94$ for CLIP on CIFAR-10$\times$STL-10)
while the bottom directions remain orthogonal
($\overline{\cos^2\theta}\approx0.01$).
The Collapse Model averages these into $\bar\alpha{=}0.52$,
predicting moderate superadditivity.
In reality, the dominant (high-$\sigma$) directions are fully redundant
and the orthogonal (low-$\sigma$) directions carry negligible energy,
so the merge provides almost no diversity gain ($\Delta r_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}31$
vs.\ ResNet-50's $\Delta r_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}282$ on the same pair).

This analysis identifies a precise \emph{applicability boundary}:
the Collapse Model is reliable when the principal-angle distribution
is approximately uniform (supervised encoders, within-domain pairs),
but fails when it is bimodal (self-supervised encoders on cross-domain pairs).
A natural fix---replacing the uniform average $\bar\alpha=\SAk$ with
an \emph{energy-weighted} alignment
$\bar\alpha_w=\sum_i({\sigma_i}/{\|H\|_*})\cos^2\theta_i$---would
down-weight orthogonal low-energy directions and up-weight
the redundant high-energy ones, potentially restoring predictive
accuracy for self-supervised encoders.
However, our experimental validation shows that this correction
\emph{does not improve predictive accuracy}:
on CLIP ($n{=}61$), the uniform $\SAk$ yields Pearson $r{=}0.951$,
MAE${=}206$, while $\bar\alpha_w$ yields $r{=}0.935$, MAE${=}218$ (worse);
DINO shows a similar pattern ($r$ drops from $0.912$ to $0.904$,
MAE increases by $4\%$);
ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 also show no improvement.
A possible explanation is that simple energy weighting over-emphasises
the first singular direction's redundancy
($\cos^2\theta_1{\approx}0.87$ for that direction),
while improperly suppressing the estimated contribution of
intermediate directions to the merge.
More refined corrections (e.g., piecewise paired-direction models,
per-direction threshold truncation, or adaptive $k$ selection)
may break through this bottleneck and are left to future work.

\subsection{E\_diverse: Cross-Architecture Probe Independence}
\label{sec:ediverse}

\textbf{Design.}
To test whether the Collapse Model's predictions are robust to the choice of encoder,
we evaluate the same 6 dataset pairs (from E1b) using both ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16
as independent probes, giving 12 total configurations.

\textbf{Results.}
ResNet-50: $R^2=0.557$, Pearson $r=0.9987$, 100\% superadditive.
ViT-B/16: $R^2=-16.1$ (systematic over-prediction), Pearson $r=0.9825$, 83.3\% superadditive.
Cross-architecture Pearson $r\geq0.982$ on these six pairs suggests similar
directional rankings, but cannot establish probe independence.
The Pearson vs.\ $R^2$ gap for ViT-B/16 shows encoder-dependent scale bias
despite stable ordering.

\subsection{E4: Cross-Probe Consistency}
\label{sec:e4}

\textbf{Design.}
We compute the $6\times6$ pairwise $\SAk$ alignment matrix $M$ independently using
ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 on 4 common datasets $\{$CIFAR-10, MNIST, STL-10, SVHN$\}$.

\subsection{E\_downstream: Effective Rank as Downstream Quality Proxy}
\label{sec:edownstream}

\textbf{Motivation.}
The Collapse Model predicts $\reff$ of merged features, but does higher $\reff$
actually correspond to better downstream performance?
We test this by computing $\reff$ and linear-probe (LP) accuracy
for 10 pretrained encoders on 5 datasets (50 total pairs).

\textbf{Design.}
Encoders: ResNet-\{50, 101\}, EfficientNet-B4, ConvNeXt-T, DenseNet-121,
ViT-B/16, DeiT-B, Swin-T, BEiT-B, RegNet-Y (covering CNN, ViT, and hybrid families).
Datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, SVHN, Fashion-MNIST.
For each (encoder, dataset) pair: extract $N{=}2000$ train features,
compute $\reff$; train LP (sklearn LogisticRegression) on train, evaluate on test.
Report per-dataset Spearman $\rho(\reff, \mathrm{LP\;acc})$ across 10 encoders.

The ``all probes'' column ($\rho\approx+0.59$ on natural images)
is \emph{confounded by feature dimension}: CNNs ($d{=}2048$) have

apparent correlation.
Controlling for dimension reveals a sharper picture:
\textbf{within the ViT family} ($d{=}768$, 5 probes),
$\reff$ is strongly predictive of LP accuracy on natural images
($\rho{=}0.90$ on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10),
while \textbf{within the CNN family} ($d{\geq}1024$, 5 probes)
the correlation is weak ($\rho{=}0.20$--$0.30$).
This suggests that $\reff$ captures useful discriminative information
primarily for architectures with compact feature spaces ($d{<}1024$),
where spectral diversity is more tightly coupled to representation quality.

\textbf{Implication for the Collapse Model.}
$\reff$ should be understood as a measure of
\emph{representational diversity}---the number of independent directions
activated by the data---rather than a direct proxy for downstream accuracy.
Collapse-4S predicts when merging may increase this spectral diversity.
Within one evaluated architecture family, diversity and accuracy are
positively correlated ($\rho{=}0.90$ for ViTs), but this association neither
establishes necessity nor guarantees that $\reff$-based selection improves a
new task.
Across architecture families or across datasets of varying difficulty,
additional task-specific metrics are needed.


\paragraph{Sample-size sensitivity.}
Empirically, the over-prediction ratio grows with $N/\reff$ on 10
representative ResNet-50 pairs:
at $N{=}200$ the mean ratio is $1.021$, rising monotonically to $1.545$ at $N{=}2000$
(Spearman $\rho{=}0.80$ between $N/\reff$ and $\log\mathrm{bias}$).
This association helps predict calibration variation across encoders
(ResNet-50 $c{=}0.78$, DINO $c{=}0.58$), but is not asserted as a
theoretical error law.
