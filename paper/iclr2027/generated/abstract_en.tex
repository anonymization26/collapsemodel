Can unlabeled feature geometry reduce the number of data-pool combinations requiring supervised evaluation? We study screening with frozen representations, separating spectral information, conditional risk models, and empirical utility. A constructive example shows that marginal singular spectra and principal angles do not determine merged effective rank. A standard Bayesian linear model motivates target-weighted A-optimal screening, but its risk identity does not extend to arbitrary conditional shift or softmax classification. We compare it with target-blind diversity and same-information second-moment matching. On PACS and Office-Home, A-optimal screening fails a prespecified 2\% relative Brier improvement threshold; exhaustive audits reveal only 0.781\% mean oracle headroom over the strongest target-blind baseline. In a subsequent equal-cost DomainNet study, 455 combinations per target each contain 1,536 source samples. Shortlisting 227 combinations retains 98.333\% of the independent-test top ten on average across six target domains and two encoders. However, second-moment MMD attains the same primary recall and all twelve validation-selected combinations. Moreover, the entire within-task relative Brier span is below 0.425\%, making a 5\% regret threshold uninformative. These results support a bounded role for target-conditioned second-order geometry in pre-screening, not a superior A-optimal selector or a universal label-free utility law. We provide auditable manifests, rankings, and exhaustive outcome tables.
